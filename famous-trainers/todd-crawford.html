Education & Early Career

Findlay College, Dale Wilkinson, and the Foundation of a Reined Cow Horse Dynasty

Todd Crawford grew up with a lifelong passion for horses, competing as a youth before making the decision to formalize his education in the discipline he loved. He enrolled at Findlay College in Ohio — and graduated in the first class of students to receive a Bachelor of Science degree from the equestrian program established by legendary horseman Dale Wilkinson. That inaugural class set a standard; Crawford was part of it from the start.

After graduating, he returned to Findlay as an instructor — teaching the next generation of students in the program that had trained him. From Findlay, he apprenticed with Kyle Quarter Horses before establishing his own training facility in California, where he built his early reputation as a reined cow horse and reining specialist. Eventually, he and his wife Pam relocated to Blanchard, Oklahoma, where they established Crawford Performance Horses and raised their daughters Carter and MacKinnon. It is in Blanchard, in the rolling country south of Oklahoma City, that Crawford's career reached its historic heights.

Training horses is choosing to work 20-hour days so we never have to work an eight-hour day.

— Todd Crawford

His philosophy in the arena is equally direct: "I believe the stop is all about how well you can run. If you don't run well your chances of stopping certainly diminish." And on milestones: "If you concentrate on the horses, these kinds of milestones take care of themselves. You can't do it without the horses and the people involved." The man who became the NRCHA's first $2 million rider has never appeared to be chasing the number — only the horsemanship that produced it.

Both the $1 million and $2 million milestones were crossed at the NRCHA Snaffle Bit Futurity — the premier event in reined cow horse, held each year in Reno, Nevada. That both milestones arrived at that event is not coincidence. The Snaffle Bit Futurity is the highest-stakes and most prestigious event in the sport. Reaching financial milestones there is a function of consistently qualifying, consistently competing in the finals, and consistently finishing at the top of a field that includes every major name in reined cow horse.

The story of Hickorys Holly Cee encapsulates what makes Crawford special: the ability to develop a horse across multiple disciplines at the highest level, and the patience to stick with a horse through both triumph and heartbreak. In 1997, she was the leader after the preliminary go-rounds at the NRCHA Snaffle Bit Futurity — and then fell during the fence work while circling in the finals. No one who watched that run forgot it. Crawford kept training her, and the mare won everything that followed.

He rode her to the 1999 River Front NRCHA Derby Championship ($50,000), the 2000 USEF Reining Championship, AQHA World Championship titles, and countless derby wins. He also rode her to the finals of both the NRCHA Snaffle Bit Futurity and the NRHA Futurity — a rare cross-discipline double. When she was sold to Arcese Enterprise of Weatherford, Texas in 1999, Crawford continued as her trainer. Her daughter ARC Hollys Chicadee later became the 2006 NRCHA Derby Non-Pro Champion. The next generation carried the excellence forward.

2006 — The Championship Year

Smart Crackin Chic and the Snaffle Bit Futurity Title

In 2006, Todd Crawford won the NRCHA Snaffle Bit Futurity Open Championship aboard Smart Crackin Chic — and in the same event crossed the $1 million threshold in NRCHA career earnings. The dual milestone made it one of the defining events of his career. The same year, he held the No. 1 spot in the NRCHA Open standings, a ranking he also held in 2007. Between 2006 and 2008, his winnings made him the all-time leading money winner in reined cow horse competition.

Beyond the competitive record, Crawford's crossover into reining is worth noting. He won more than $900,000 in NRHA competition across his career — riding some of the same horses at both the NRHA Futurity and the NRCHA Snaffle Bit Futurity in the same year. Key reining horses include NRHA Hall of Famer Princess In Diamonds, Matt Dillon Dun It, Commandalena (multiple AQHA Reserve World Champion Reining), Lena Gallo, and Mister Nicadual — the same horse that competed for Tim McQuay at the 2006 WEG Aachen. He won the FEI World Reining Masters, a European-based international title, and competed at USEF events across his career.

Leadership & Service

Three-Time NRCHA President — Shaping the Sport Beyond the Show Pen

Todd Crawford's contribution to the NRCHA extends far beyond his competitive record. He has served multiple terms as NRCHA President, as a member of the Board of Directors, and as an active supporter of the NRCHA Youth program. His service to the association that made him its most decorated earner is characteristic of a person who understands that the health of the sport depends on people giving back to it — not just drawing prize money from it.

Crawford and his students have won more than three dozen World Championship titles across three major performance horse associations (NRCHA, NRHA, and AQHA). His coaching and mentoring have shaped many of the current top open riders in reined cow horse. He is also a respected clinician, offering hands-on instruction known for its practical emphasis on fundamentals — the same stop-by-running philosophy that built his own horses. NRCHA Executive Director Emily Konkel said at the time of his $3 million milestone: "We're fortunate to have many individuals like Todd who represent our sport on a high level with integrity."
