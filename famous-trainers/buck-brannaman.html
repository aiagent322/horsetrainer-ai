Background

Buck Brannaman

Dan M. "Buck" Brannaman was born on January 29, 1962, in Sheboygan, Wisconsin, and was raised in Montana and Idaho. His father taught him and his brother trick roping from an early age and they performed at rodeos and fairs as "The Idaho Cowboys, Buckshot and Smokie." Brannaman had a difficult childhood marked by physical abuse at the hands of his father, and he and his brother spent years in foster care. After being placed with a foster family he graduated high school at 17 and began working under his mentor Ray Hunt. He is married to Mary Brannaman and they have three daughters. He is based in Sheridan, Wyoming. In September 2020 he was kicked in the head by a horse at his ranch, suffered life-threatening skull fractures and brain bleeding, underwent emergency surgery, and was placed in a medically induced coma.

Career and Philosophy

Natural Horsemanship

Brannaman trained for many years under Ray Hunt and was also influenced by Tom and Bill Dorrance. His philosophy is based on classical concepts from the vaquero tradition — working with the horse's nature, using an understanding of how horses think and communicate. He has said the goal of his clinics "is really just to try to get the human being to understand as much about their horse as I can help them to understand." He has started over 10,000 young colts and teaches clinics annually in the United States, Europe, Australia, and New Zealand.

He served as the principal equine consultant for the 1998 film The Horse Whisperer and worked closely with director Robert Redford. The 2011 documentary Buck, about his life, won the Sundance Film Festival U.S. Documentary Competition Audience Award. He co-authored the autobiography The Faraway Horses with William Reynolds. He has also written Groundwork, Believe, and Ranch Roping.
