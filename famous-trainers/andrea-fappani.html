Early Life

Bergamo, Italy — A Dairy Farm, a Pony, and a Dream Nobody Around Him Understood

Andrea Fappani was born and raised in Bergamo, in the Lombardy region of northern Italy, the son of Sergio and Maddalisa Fappani — fourth-generation dairy farmers. Everything in his world pointed toward the farm, toward the cows, toward a life in Bergamo. But horses had a different plan.

"My dad introduced me to horses when I was about four years old, and I began taking lessons," Fappani has recalled. "He wanted me to have a hobby that we could share together." At the time, western performance equine events were almost non-existent in Europe. The riding world around Fappani was English — jumping, dressage, the disciplines that dominate the continent. He took to jumping quickly, spending his first two or three years in an English saddle. Then a friend showed him something different.

"He told us there was something amazing that we needed to check out," Fappani said. That something was reining. He was nine years old when he first saw it, and the precision, athleticism, and controlled power of the sliding stop hit him in a way that nothing else had. He switched disciplines immediately and never looked back. Western performance events barely existed in Europe at that point. He was building toward something he could not yet name in a country where nobody around him had any idea what it was.

The thing I love about the States is the chances people gave me. In Europe, it's more about politics, who you are, and what kind of connections you have. Here, it's about what you can do.

— Andrea Fappani
Italy to America

The Italian Futurity, Todd Bergen, and the Move to Oregon

By his teens, Fappani had become a standout Non-Pro reiner in the European circuit, winning the Italian Futurity in 1996. That victory was pivotal not just for the title but for what it brought: in approximately 1995, Todd Bergen — then a rising NRHA star who would go on to become the first rider to earn $2 million in two different associations — had attended the Italian Futurity and watched a young Italian ride.

Bergen saw something. He offered the young man a job if he ever wanted to come to the United States. "I felt he saw something in me, and maybe there was something more in the back of my mind," Fappani later recalled. Bergen's recollection is direct: "Andrea was so focused, and reining was all he thought about. He was very serious about what he was doing. It doesn't surprise me at all he has gone on to be so successful. He is one of the most driven people I have ever been around."

After winning the Italian Futurity and receiving his parents' blessing, Fappani moved to the United States — settling in Scottsdale, Arizona, then making his way to Bergen's operation in Oregon. In 1997, he captured the Limited Non Pro Championship at the NRHA Futurity in Oklahoma City aboard Best Buy. That same year he met Tish Wood, who would become his wife and lifelong partner. He then spent four formative years working for Bergen — immersing himself in every aspect of elite horsemanship, including mandatory military service in Italy during which he and Tish maintained a long-distance relationship.

While still working under Bergen, Fappani made his professional debut in 2001. He won the NRHA Open Futurity — the richest, most prestigious event in reining — on RR Star. He was the youngest competitor and the first European in history to win it. He became a US citizen in 2006. He and Tish set up their own training facility in 2002. The career was launched.

The Championship Record

Four Futurities, Nine Million Dollars, and the Records That Keep Falling

No one in the history of reining has earned what Andrea Fappani has earned. He is the NRHA's all-time leading money earner — the first $7 Million Rider, the first $8 Million Rider, the first $9 Million Rider. Each milestone has been the first time any rider in history reached it. As of 2024, his lifetime earnings exceed $9 million, and he was inducted into the NRHA Hall of Fame that same year — formally recognized as among the greatest in the sport's history while still actively competing at its highest levels.

The foundation of those earnings is four NRHA Open Futurity Championships — the most coveted title in reining — on four different horses across more than a decade of elite competition:

Beyond the Arena

Teacher, Clinician, Television, and the Fappani Saddle

Andrea Fappani's influence extends well beyond the show pen. He is a respected clinician and teacher, sharing the philosophy that has driven his career — that champions are made in preparation, not performance; that excellence is daily, not occasional. He hosts the Along For The Ride with Andrea Fappani podcast, carrying his competitive philosophy and career wisdom to a global audience.

He has appeared on the hit television series Yellowstone and The Last Cowboy — bringing the world of elite reining to a mainstream audience in a way that few professionals in the discipline have achieved. His presence on those platforms has introduced the sport to millions of viewers who otherwise would never have encountered a sliding stop.

His collaboration with Bob's Custom Saddles produced the "Fappani Saddle" — designed from decades of real-world competitive experience and built around his uncompromising standards for balance, close contact, rider position, and freedom of movement. It is now the #1 highest-selling saddle model Bob's Custom Saddles produces, trusted by professionals and non-pros across western performance disciplines worldwide.

Champions do not become champions when they win the event, but in the hours, weeks, months and years they spend preparing for it. The victorious performance itself is merely the demonstration of the championship character.

— Andrea Fappani

Fappani's wife Tish is an integral part of Fappani Performance Horses — an NRHA Million Dollar Rider and Breeders status holder who brings the same discipline and drive to the operation. Their son Luca is building his own reining career, already with substantial NRHA earnings. At Fappani Performance Horses in Scottsdale, Arizona, the standard is the same standard it has always been: preparation, precision, and the relentless pursuit of the championship character that was formed on a dairy farm in Bergamo before anyone outside Italy had heard the name.
