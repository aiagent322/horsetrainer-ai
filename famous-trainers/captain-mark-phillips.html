Background

Captain Mark Phillips

Mark Anthony Peter Phillips was born on September 22, 1948, in Tetbury, Gloucestershire, England. His father was Major Peter William Garside Phillips MC; his mother was Anne Patricia Phillips (née Tiarks). He attended Stouts Hill Preparatory School, then Marlborough College, then the Royal Military Academy Sandhurst. He was commissioned as a Second Lieutenant in the 1st The Queen's Dragoon Guards in July 1969, promoted to Lieutenant in January 1971, and was an acting Captain by the time of his marriage in November 1973. He was officially promoted to Captain in July 1975 and retired from the Army on March 30, 1978. He was appointed Personal aide-de-camp to Queen Elizabeth II. His full title is Captain Mark Anthony Peter Phillips CVO ADC(P).

He married Princess Anne, daughter of Queen Elizabeth II, at Westminster Abbey on November 14, 1973. He was offered an earldom upon the marriage and declined. They had two children: Peter Phillips (born 1977) and Zara Phillips (born 1981, now Zara Tindall). The couple separated in 1989 and divorced on April 28, 1992. He married dressage rider Sandy Pflueger — who represented the United States at the 1984 Los Angeles Olympics — on February 1, 1997; they have a daughter, Stephanie. The couple separated in 2012.

Competition Career

Competitive Record

Phillips was a reserve member of the British equestrian team at the 1968 Mexico City Olympics. He was a member of the British three-day event teams that won the World Championship in 1970 (Punchestown, on Chicago), the European Championship in 1971 (Burghley, on Great Ovation), and Olympic Team Gold at the 1972 Munich Games (on Great Ovation). He was also a member of the British team that won the Team Silver at the 1988 Seoul Olympics (riding Cartier; his horse sustained a pulled muscle and could not complete the individual event). He won the Badminton Horse Trials four times: 1971 and 1972 on Great Ovation, 1974 on Columbus, and 1981 on Lincoln. He won the Burghley Horse Trials in 1973 on Maid Marion. He also represented Great Britain on two winning Nations Cup show jumping teams. He retired from competition in 1988.

Coaching and Course Design

US Chef d'Equipe and Course Design

Phillips served as Chef d'Equipe and Technical Advisor for the United States Eventing Team from 1992 to 2012. Under his leadership the US team never returned from a major championship without at least one medal, across four Olympic Games. His US team results include: 1996 Atlanta Olympics (Team Silver, Individual Bronze); 2000 Sydney Olympics (Individual Gold, Team Bronze); 2002 WEG Team Gold; 2004 Athens Olympics (Individual Silver, Team Bronze); 2008 Hong Kong Olympics (Individual Silver). He stepped down after the 2012 London Olympics. He was inducted into the USEA Eventing Hall of Fame in 2018.

He is a course designer for major eventing competitions including Burghley CCI4* (over 20 times since 1989), Luhmuhlen CCI4* (12 years), the Festival of British Eventing at Gatcombe Park (also chairman), and the 2018 World Equestrian Games in Tryon, North Carolina. He is a regular columnist for Horse & Hound magazine. His daughter Zara Tindall won the 2006 World Equestrian Games individual gold medal in eventing; he was her trainer. Zara also won a team silver medal at the 2012 London Olympics.
