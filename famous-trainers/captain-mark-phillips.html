Tetbury to Sandhurst — The Making of a Captain

A Military Family, Marlborough College, and the Cavalry Regiment That Made Room for Horses

Mark Anthony Peter Phillips was born September 22, 1948, in Tetbury, Gloucestershire — the heart of the English countryside, a county of stone walls, hunt country, and horses. His father was Major Peter William Garside Phillips MC; his mother Anne Patricia (née Tiarks) came from a military family with a lineage that included aides-de-camp to the Crown. He attended Stouts Hill Preparatory School and Marlborough College before entering the Royal Military Academy Sandhurst — the training ground for the British Army's officer corps.

Commissioned as a Second Lieutenant in the 1st The Queen's Dragoon Guards in July 1969, he was promoted to Lieutenant in January 1971 and was an acting Captain by the time he married Princess Anne in November 1973. He was officially promoted to Captain in July 1975 and retired from the Army on March 30, 1978. He was appointed a Personal aide-de-camp to Queen Elizabeth II. His full title: Captain Mark Anthony Peter Phillips CVO ADC(P). He has been known as "Captain Mark Phillips" ever since — the designation is traditional for retired cavalry captains who work with horses.

Captain Mark Phillips has overseen the most successful period in the history of US eventing, leading them to 23 team and individual medals — never returning from a major event without at least one medal.

— USEA, on Phillips's tenure as US Chef d'Equipe

His equestrian talent was apparent early. As a reserve member of the British equestrian team at the 1968 Mexico City Olympics, he watched the sport from the outside. What followed was two decades of competition at the highest level — and then two more decades reshaping it from the other side of the fence.

The Competition Career — 1970 to 1988

World Gold, Olympic Gold, and the King of Badminton

From 1970 to 1988, Mark Phillips was one of the finest event riders in the world. He represented Great Britain on teams that won at every major level of the sport — and as an individual, he collected four Badminton Horse Trials titles across a span of ten years, a record of consistency that very few have matched.

The 1972 Munich Olympics was the defining team performance. Phillips rode Great Ovation — the same horse who had won Badminton with him in 1971 and 1972 — to a team gold medal with Great Britain. He placed 35th individually. Sixteen years later, at the 1988 Seoul Olympics, his horse Cartier sustained a pulled muscle and could not complete the individual three-day event. The British team still won the silver medal. Phillips retired from competition in 1988. His record: two Olympic medals, two World Championship medals, one European Championship gold, four Badminton titles, and one Burghley title. He also represented Great Britain on two winning Nations Cup show jumping teams.

Princess Anne — Westminster Abbey, 1973

The Marriage That Made Him Part of the Royal Family

Mark Phillips met Princess Anne — the only daughter of Queen Elizabeth II and Prince Philip, Duke of Edinburgh — through their shared world of competitive eventing. The Princess Royal was herself an accomplished event rider; she competed at the 1976 Montreal Olympics for Great Britain and was European champion in 1971. They were peers in the sport before they were anything else.

They married on November 14, 1973, at Westminster Abbey — one of the most-watched royal weddings of the era. The couple had two children: Peter Phillips (born 1977) and Zara Phillips (born 1981, now Zara Tindall), who inherited her parents' equestrian gifts and went further than either of them in the sport. Mark was offered an earldom upon his marriage — a traditional offer to the husband of a senior royal — and declined.

The marriage strained through the 1980s. The couple separated in 1989. In 1991, following court-mandated DNA testing, it was confirmed that Phillips had fathered a daughter named Felicity in 1985 during an affair with New Zealand art teacher Heather Tonkin. Mark and Princess Anne divorced on April 28, 1992. Through his US eventing work in the 1980s, Phillips had met dressage rider Sandy Pflueger, who had represented the United States at the 1984 Los Angeles Olympics. They married on February 1, 1997, and have a daughter, Stephanie. The couple separated in 2012.

23+ team and individual medals · Four Olympic Games · Never once returned empty-handed

When Mark Phillips became Chef d'Equipe of the United States eventing team in the early 1990s, American eventing was competitive but had not reached the consistent podium presence it would achieve under his two decades of leadership. What followed was the most successful sustained run in the history of US eventing — four Olympic Games, multiple World Equestrian Games, and Pan American Games cycles, and in every single major championship, at least one medal came home with the team.

The 2000 Sydney Olympics individual gold — won with the US team — was perhaps the singular coaching achievement of his tenure. Across 1999, 2003, and 2007, the Pan American Games yielded team gold each time, plus a consistent flow of individual medals. He stepped down from the Chef d'Equipe role following the 2012 London Olympics, after twenty years. The USEA inducted him into its Eventing Hall of Fame in 2018.

Zara Tindall — Father, Trainer, World Champion's Parent

The Daughter Who Won the World Championship

Zara Phillips — born May 15, 1981, to Mark Phillips and Princess Anne — grew up with one of the finest equestrian minds in the world as her father and trainer. The equestrian genetics were not accidental: her mother was a European champion and Olympian; her father a four-time Badminton champion and Olympic gold medalist. She did not waste the inheritance.

In 2006, Zara Phillips won the individual gold medal at the World Equestrian Games — the world eventing championship. Mark Phillips was her trainer. In 2012, she won a team silver medal at the London Olympics with Great Britain — competing in the same city where her parents had married at Westminster Abbey in 1973. She is now Zara Tindall (married to rugby player Mike Tindall in 2011). The Phillips family contribution to British eventing spans three generations of competition at the highest level.

Course Design & Legacy

Burghley, Luhmuhlen, Tryon — Building the Stages Where Champions Are Made

Alongside his coaching career, Mark Phillips became one of the most respected cross-country course designers in the world. Designing a cross-country eventing course requires a specific kind of expertise: the ability to challenge the best horse-and-rider combinations in the world while maintaining safety standards and the integrity of the sport. Phillips has designed the Burghley CCI4* course — one of the most prestigious in eventing — more than twenty times since 1989, with a continuing design role. He also designed the Luhmuhlen CCI4* course in Germany for twelve years.

In the United States, he designed the USEA American Eventing Championships and multiple events at the Tryon International Equestrian Center. He served as course designer for the 2018 World Equestrian Games in Tryon, North Carolina — the first WEG held in the United States. At Gatcombe Park, he is both course designer and chairman of the Festival of British Eventing. He has served as a regular columnist for Horse & Hound, Britain's oldest equestrian weekly magazine, for decades.
