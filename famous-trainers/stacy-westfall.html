Background

Stacy Westfall

Stacy Westfall was born on September 26, 1974, in South China, Maine. Her parents, Sherri and Biff Gliddon, were not horse professionals, though her mother Sherri had a lifelong love of horses. When Stacy was six years old the family got their first pony, named Midnight Misty, the day after she broke her arm falling off a friend's pony. Her father later gave her her first full-sized horse, a mare named Bay, as a reward for good grades; she used Bay for barrel racing. Her mother was her only instructor until she went to college. She attended the University of Findlay in Ohio, which has one of the top equestrian studies programs in the world. On a school field trip to the All American Quarter Horse Congress she watched freestyle reining for the first time. She began pursuing bridleless riding after accidentally dropping a rein during a traditional reining competition. She is married to Jesse Westfall and they have three children.

She has said: "The bond is the same for me with or without the bridle. Without the bridle, other people see the bond more. But nothing changes between the horse and me."

On bridleless riding: "Achieve a very high level of training on a loose rein for a long time. If you don't run into problems, you haven't done it long enough. When you can fix problems without the reins, you are getting closer."

Career

Competitive Record

In 2003 Westfall won the NRHA Futurity Freestyle Championship riding bridleless. In 2006 she was the first woman to compete in and win the Road to the Horse colt-starting competition. Also in 2006, she won the All American Quarter Horse Congress Freestyle Reining competition riding both bridleless and bareback on her black Quarter Horse mare Whizards Baby Doll, known as Roxy. She performed to the song "Live Like You Were Dying" by Tim McGraw and dedicated the ride to her father, who had died 24 days before her performance. According to an interview in Equine Wellness magazine, the bridleless and bareback partnership with Roxy required approximately 1,000 hours of training: 800 hours to achieve reliable bridleless riding and 200 hours to ride the full reining pattern bareback. In February 2008 a video of the 2006 Congress ride was posted to her website and went viral. On March 14, 2008 she appeared on The Ellen DeGeneres Show with Roxy. Roxy died in 2012. Westfall was inducted into the National Cowgirl Museum and Hall of Fame in 2012. She operates Westfall Horsemanship, hosts a podcast, and conducts clinics worldwide.
