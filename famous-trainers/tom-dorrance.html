Background

Tom Dorrance

Tom Dorrance was born on May 11, 1910, near Joseph, Oregon, and grew up on the family's Crow Creek ranch northeast of Enterprise, Oregon. He died on June 11, 2003, at age 93. His brother was Bill Dorrance (January 19, 1906 – July 20, 1999). He married his wife Margaret in 1966. He grew up in the Great Basin buckaroo tradition. He and his brother Bill are considered among the founders of the modern natural horsemanship movement.

He called himself "the horse's lawyer" and "the horse's advocate." He has said: "What I know about the horse I learned from the horse." He has also said: "The thing you are trying to help the horse do is to use his own mind. You are trying to present something and then let him figure out how to get there." He frequently posed the question: "what happened, before what happened happened?" He based his methods on feel, timing, and balance. He has said: "This is something I have had to develop in myself, for myself, by myself. The True Unity and Willing Communication between the horse and me is not something that can be handed to someone, it has to be learned."

In 1960 he sold his Oregon ranch and began traveling throughout the West, working with horses and sharing his knowledge. He avoided media attention and clinics while his best-known student, Ray Hunt, was giving clinics far and wide from the mid-1970s. He also influenced Buck Brannaman, Sheila Varian, Pat Parelli, and many others. He lived simply and frugally and never capitalized on the natural horsemanship movement.

Recognition and Publications

Legacy

In 1994 the NRCHA honored Dorrance with the Vaquero Award. In 1995 the Cowboy Hall of Fame presented him with the Chester A. Reynolds Memorial Award, recognizing him as a person whose lifestyle exemplified the ideals of the American West. In 1999 he received the Lavin Cup from the American Association of Equine Practitioners. His book, True Unity: Willing Communication Between Horse and Human, edited by Milly Hunt Porter, was first published by Word Dancer Press in 1987 and has been in continuous print since.
