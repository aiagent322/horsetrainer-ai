The Wright Family — San Juan Bautista

Born to a California Cow Horse Legacy — 40 Mares, Legendary Stallions, a Father Like Walter Wright

Justin Wright was born into one of reined cow horse's most storied California families. His father, Walter Wright, is a legend in the sport — the man who bred the stallions Smokum Oak and Smokums Prize, the latter of which won more money in reined cow horse competition than any horse of his era. The Wright family's ranch at San Juan Canyon Road in San Juan Bautista (Benito County, near Hollister) housed 40 to 60 broodmares at any given time. Growing up in that environment was an education no classroom could replicate.

"My parents were really good to me," Justin has said. "Being able to sneak one of those colts out and get to learn on starting those colts was an advantage to learning." His father first took him to an NRCHA show to compete at age fourteen. By sixteen, at the 2002 NRCHA Snaffle Bit Futurity at Reno, Justin came home with three belt buckles — the same night his father Walter was named NRCHA Stock Horseman of the Year, a lifetime achievement honor. Walter, sitting at the awards banquet slicing his meat, had no idea the award was coming. "I was really shocked," he said. "I was honored and shocked both." Justin, chuckling at the memory: "He had not one clue."

What makes Justin Wright's path distinctive in an era of professional apprenticeships is that he never worked outside his family. He had mentors — he still rides with Todd Bergen when possible — but the training program he built, the horses he developed, the owners he attracted, were built by one person from one California ranch. "His DNA is horses," a profile noted simply. After a successful non-pro career, he took his open card in 2009 and hasn't stopped. Between non-pro and open careers, he has shown a futurity horse every year for more than twenty years.

Building the Career — 2009 to 2020

From "Those Premier Events Look Impossible" to Multiple Championships

Justin's non-pro career at the Futurity was strong: 2006 Int Non-Pro Reserve Champion and Non-Pro 3rd on Smart Prize; 2007 Non-Pro Reserve Champion on Talkin Prize. When he turned professional in 2009, the curve was immediately visible. In 2010 he was the NRCHA Intermediate Open Reserve Champion on No Chic Flicks. By 2017, his first Premier Event championship arrived.

Lil Bay Hawk — a 2012 gelding by Catty Hawk out of Little Gray Freckles, owned by Stephen Silva — gave Wright his breakthrough at the 2017 NRCHA Derby Open. "It was a weight off my shoulders, I guess you could say. Those premier events as an up-and-coming trainer, they look impossible to win. So it was gratifying. It meant a lot to me, getting that first one. It gives you a sort of confidence that you can compete with these people. It was definitely a confidence boost." That same year, he placed Reserve at the Snaffle Bit Futurity Open on Shiners Diamond Cat — a horse with more titles ahead of them.

Scooter Kat was the horse who carried Wright into the Million Dollar Rider club — winning the 2019 and 2020 NRCHA Stakes Open titles plus the 2020 NRCHA Derby Open in the same calendar year. At the 2019 NRCHA Celebration of Champions, Wright crossed the million-dollar mark. "It was a relief because that was one of the goals and dreams I had; something I always wanted. He was now in the same peer group as the trainers he'd looked up to his entire career."

2021 — Zak 34 and the Loudest Futurity in Years

"I've Lost It Probably More Than Anybody in the Last Four Years"

2021 NRCHA Snaffle Bit Futurity Open Champion
Zak 34
Metallic Cat × Cat Digs Lucinda (High Brow Cat) · Black Stallion · Fischer Family
226
Fence Score · Will Rogers Coliseum · Fort Worth, Texas · October 23, 2021
"I've had dreams of this for as long as I can remember. It's consumed my life to win the Snaffle Bit Futurity... This is the first year that winning the Snaffle Bit Futurity didn't consume every thought that I had, and maybe that's why it's so emotional."

The 2021 NRCHA Snaffle Bit Futurity Open finals might go down as the loudest and most intense in many years. No clear front runner emerged until the last set of cow work. Then came Wright and the jet-black stallion Zak 34, and a score of 226 down the fence.

Zak 34 came to Wright through his wife Gloria. At the sale where the Fischer family bought the black colt, Wright was in attendance. Gloria urged him to approach the family about training plans for their new horse. "It turns out they didn't have a trainer in mind for the colt," and after some conversation decided to send Zak 34 to California with Wright. What followed was a horse that gave Wright a special feeling from the beginning — but, as he acknowledged, he had felt that feeling before and still not won the Futurity. He had been Reserve at the Futurity with Scooter Kat in 2018. Close, but not there. "I've been here, and I've lost it probably more than anybody in the last four years."

The 2021 win changed that. After the buzzer sounded on the 226, the arena erupted. Four horses in the finals; the title on the first — "I realized that I had won it." Wright's brother Greg had won the Snaffle Bit Futurity before him. Now Justin had too. "It's emotional, but it's just everything I've ever dreamed of."
