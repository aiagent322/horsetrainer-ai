Early Life — Ohio Roots, Arizona Horizons

From Ohio to Scottsdale — A Career Built on Consistency and One Defining Record

Craig Schmersal was born and raised in Ohio. His path to professional reining began the way many do — through a parent's love of horses. His father bought a couple of two-year-olds and sent them to trainer Mike Flarida — the same Mike Flarida who would win two NRHA Open Futurity Championships and enter the NRHA Hall of Fame. Schmersal credits his father's drive and love for horses as foundational to everything that followed, and credits the summers spent riding with Mike Flarida as an integral key to his success.

He began his professional career in California — initially based in Menifee, California, where he became the only California trainer ranked in the NRHA Top 25 Lifetime Money Earnings list. He eventually relocated to Scottsdale, Arizona, where Schmersal Reining Horses operates today alongside his wife Ginger and their family. He earned his first NRHA check in 1988: $61.38. He has now earned over $5 million.

Training horses was something I was going to do whether I made money or not. I just wanted to ride horses. I never thought in a million years I'd make a million dollars.

— Craig Schmersal

The person Schmersal reserves his highest praise for is his wife Ginger. "She's my eyes," he has said. "She's just been great for my business and great for me." Ginger is both a business partner and a competitive non-pro rider in her own right, with lifetime earnings approaching half a million dollars. Their daughter Addisyn also competes at a high level. The Schmersal family is a reining household — and one of the most recognizable family units in the sport, in part because of their prominent roles in The Last Cowboy.

The Defining Achievement — All Five Majors

The Only Rider in History to Win the Level 4 Open of Every Major Event

Craig Schmersal holds a distinction that no other rider in the history of professional reining can claim: he is the only person to have won the Level 4 Open Championship of all five of the sport's major events — the NRHA Futurity, the NRHA Derby, the National Reining Breeders Classic, the All-American Quarter Horse Congress, and The Run for a Million. Add to that the Americana in Germany, and Schmersal is also the only rider to have won every major international and domestic reining title.

The 2017 NRHA Futurity title — the last of the five majors to fall — is the most storied of the set. Schmersal had competed in the NRHA Futurity finals every year since 1997 without taking the top prize. For twenty-seven years he had won every other major event in the sport while the Futurity eluded him. On December 2, 2017, he entered the Jim Norick Arena last in the draw with a 224.5 to beat. His mare Face The Attitude — bred by KC Performance Horses, owned by Hickory Creek Ranch LLC (Craig and Ginger), and nominated by Ginger — delivered the run that completed the set. "After 27 Years, Craig Schmersal Wins His First NRHA Futurity" was the headline. The sport's most consistent performer had finally collected the one title he was missing.

Major Career Wins

Three Decades of Finals Appearances and Global Competition

Schmersal has not missed the NRHA Futurity finals since 1997. That streak — nearly three consecutive decades of finaling at the sport's most prestigious event — is itself a record of consistency that few trainers across any generation can claim. He has qualified multiple horses for the Open finals in the same year multiple times, reflecting the depth of his program.

His early career horse Cee Blair Sailor stands apart — an NRHA Hall of Fame inductee and one of the top money-earners in Category 1 reining. Schmersal won both the NRHA Open and Limited Open World Championship with him in 1994, and then took him to Germany for the Americana — Europe's largest NRHA event — and won that too. No American trainer had previously done it, and few have since.

Television — The Last Cowboy

Season One's Co-Champion and a Family on Camera

When Taylor Sheridan created The Last Cowboy in 2019 — the CMT/Paramount documentary-competition series that brought professional reining to a mainstream national audience for the first time at scale — Craig Schmersal was among the eight riders chosen to represent the sport. The Schmersal family featured prominently: Ginger appeared in a remarkable 24 episodes, more than any other non-riding cast member in the series, as the camera followed the full domestic reality of an elite reining household preparing for the inaugural Run for a Million.

The series culminated in the Run for a Million in Las Vegas. Schmersal rode No Smoking Required — a Colonels Smoking Gun son he had trained from the beginning of the horse's career — to a score of 229.5. Cade McCutcheon rode Custom Made Gun to the identical score. Both horses were sons of Gunner. The co-champions opted not to run off and split the $307,500 each. The moment — two riders, two Gunner sons, tied on the sport's biggest stage — became one of the most talked-about outcomes in reining's modern television era. Season 2 in 2021 continued the Schmersal family's presence, with an episode titled "The Next Generation" featuring Craig, Matt Mills, and Andrea Fappani training their children in the sport.
