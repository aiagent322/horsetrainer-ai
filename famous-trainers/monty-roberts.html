Background

Monty Roberts

Marvin Earl "Monty" Roberts MVO was born on May 14, 1935, in Salinas, California. His father, Marvin E. Roberts, was a professional horse trainer who operated a rodeo competition center in Salinas and authored a self-published book, Horse and Horseman Training, in 1957. His mother was Marguerite Roberts. Roberts won his first riding championship at age four. As a child he was Elizabeth Taylor's body double in the film National Velvet (Metro-Goldwyn-Mayer, 1944). He was also an extra and technical advisor in East of Eden (Warner Bros., 1955); James Dean was sent to the Roberts family training facility by the film's producers, and Dean and Roberts became friends.

At age 13 he was sent to Nevada to round up horses for the Salinas Rodeo Association's Wild Horse Race. There he spent hours silently observing wild mustangs interact with each other and concluded they used a discernible, predictable body language to communicate. He later termed this language "Equus." He attended California Polytechnic State University, San Luis Obispo, riding for their rodeo team. He won the NIRA Champion Team Roping in 1956 and the National NIRA Champion Bulldogger in 1957. He graduated in 1959 with a degree in animal science. He has won 11 world championships in the show ring across disciplines and has produced over 200 international stake horses in thoroughbred racing.

Join-Up and Publications

Natural Horsemanship and Legacy

Roberts developed his "Join-Up" method — a training approach in which the horse approaches the trainer voluntarily, establishing trust — based on his observations of wild mustang behavior. He founded Join-Up International to promote his methods. He was appointed Member of the Royal Victorian Order (MVO). Queen Elizabeth II enlisted his training talents and has called his methods "incredible." He runs an Equestrian Academy in Solvang, California and an online university. His best-selling book is The Man Who Listens to Horses (Random House, 1997). Other books include Shy Boy: The Horse That Came In from the Wild (HarperCollins, 1999) and Horse Sense for People (Viking, 2001).
