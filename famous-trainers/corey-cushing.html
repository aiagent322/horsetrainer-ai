Origins — Utah to the Top

Noel Skinner, John Slack, and the Making of a Champion at 19

Corey Cushing grew up in Utah riding horses from childhood — a state not traditionally associated with NRCHA's top competitive circuit, but one that produced a horseman who would go on to become arguably the most decorated active competitor in the sport. The man who shaped his early horsemanship was Noel Skinner — a respected Utah horseman and family friend who passed away in 2010. Skinner's influence was both technical and personal: it was he who encouraged the teenage Cushing to pursue his dreams beyond Utah.

Right out of high school in 1998, at Skinner's urging, Cushing took an assistant trainer job with leading professional John Slack. "The neat thing was, even though I could've stayed in Utah and kept working for Noel, he encouraged me to go out and ride and train with those guys," Cushing said. "He knew they could get me on some good horses, and he wanted to see me be as successful as I could." He also spent time working for Teddy Robinson and other top cow horse and reining professionals, absorbing what each had to offer.

The stars are always within reach but the better you prepare yourself the closer those stars will be.

— Corey Cushing, favorite quote

The preparation paid off quickly. In 1999, at only nineteen years old and still working as an assistant trainer, Cushing won the NRHA Futurity Intermediate Open Championship aboard Twice As Shiney (Shining Spark × Shine A Mite × Doc O Dynamite). He also qualified for the NRHA Futurity Open Finals that same year. It was the kind of debut that announces a career. He was one of very few riders to successfully compete in both the NRHA and NRCHA Futurities — sometimes on the same horse in the same year. By age 23, he had launched his own training operation. He and his wife Kristen eventually settled in Scottsdale, Arizona, where Corey Cushing Performance Horses operates today with their children Caleb, Lincoln, and Capri.

No active competitor in the NRCHA has won the Snaffle Bit Futurity and the World's Greatest Horseman each three times. The six Premier Event championships span fifteen years of competition at the highest level, across six different horses, for multiple different owners. Each requires a completely different horse to be at its best at exactly the right moment — and in three cases, Cushing had four horses qualified for the same finals, any one of which might have won. That kind of depth, consistently maintained across a decade and a half, is what separates him from the field.

The 2018 Snaffle Bit Futurity deserves special note. SJR Diamond Mist — nicknamed "Punk" — was a son of CD Diamond, the horse who had won the Futurity with Cushing in 2012. Riding the champion's offspring to another championship, six years later, is a thread that connects two titles through one bloodline. When Cushing crossed the finish with a 222.5 fence score to bring his composite to 658.5 and win by two points, he also collected a $25,000 bonus for riding the highest-placing CD Diamond offspring. "It's amazing," Cushing said. "Kristen told me right before I left: 'You always have good luck on even years.' She was right."

Hott Rod — The Horse He Always Knew

Three Years of Planning, One Perfect Night in Fort Worth

The 2022 World's Greatest Horseman
Hott Rod
Hottish × Sugars Smart Kitty × High Brow Cat · Owned by Lynne Wurzer
"If anyone knows me, they know Hott Rod. And they know how much he means to me and how I've been thinking about this event for a long time, since I bought him as a 2-year-old."
Bought as a 2-year-old from Austin Adams · Ended 3rd at the Snaffle Bit Futurity · 2nd at the NRCHA Derby
Won the NRCHA Bridle in Idaho the fall before the 2022 WGH
2022 WGH cow work: "Feel Invincible" playing as Cushing and Hott Rod worked a charolais steer — the horse stayed perfectly in sync through every turn, every circle, to the fence in both directions
Won the 2022 World's Greatest Horseman by 4 points over Phillip Ralls and Call Me Mitch · $100,000

The 2022 World's Greatest Horseman final at Will Rogers Memorial Center in Fort Worth was one of those moments in a career that trainers describe as a culmination. Cushing had envisioned Hott Rod as a World's Greatest Horseman horse since the day he bought him as a two-year-old. The stallion had been close — third at the Snaffle Bit, second at the Derby — before winning the Idaho Bridle the fall before the 2022 event. When the buzzer sounded after the fence work, Cushing whirled his hat into the air. His assistant trainer Isaac Carrel, who had traveled every mile of the journey with the horse, was outside the gate. The emotion was visible from every seat in the arena.

On Hott Rod: "It wouldn't be fair to say he's the best because times change and horses come and go. But he is something special. He ranks really, really high and is truly something special." Coming from a man who has ridden six Premier Event champions, that is as high a compliment as a horse receives.
