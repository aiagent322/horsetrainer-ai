The Rice Family — Texas Panhandle Roots

Born to Ride — Spearman, Texas and Three Generations of Cutting Excellence

Boyd Rice was born into one of Western performance horse sport's most remarkable families. His father, Sonny Rice, was a top cutting horse trainer who won an NCHA World Championship on Jazzote. His uncles Raymond and Ronnie Rice are NCHA Hall of Famers. His cousin Tag Rice is an NCHA Hall of Famer. His brother Matlock Rice earns his living as a cutting horse trainer. "It was first place or nothing for me," Sonny Rice said of the family's competitive makeup. "All of the Rices have been real aggressive, sometimes too aggressive." Both of his boys were hard workers, Sonny acknowledged — that's all they knew.

Born in 1965, Boyd moved from Kansas to Brenham, Texas at age five when his father relocated to train horses in the Lone Star state. By age seven he was showing in youth cutting contests. He rode with his father every day until he left home at approximately twenty-one. The education was complete, even if the competitive career was still building.

He met Halee Reed, married her, and the young couple moved into a trailer home in Corsicana, Texas where Boyd trained horses for his father. When Halee's parents located property near their cattle ranch in Spearman, Texas — in the upper reaches of the Texas Panhandle — Boyd and Halee made the move. For fifteen years, far from Fort Worth and the nucleus of the NCHA, Boyd mixed ranch work with starting colts and showing a few cutting horses, building a reputation for sending home well-broke two-year-olds. The quality of horses entrusted to him steadily improved. By 1998, Spearman didn't seem so far from Fort Worth.

That's all I've ever done, actually. I rode with my dad every day. And when I marked a 74 he'd tell me I did good — but he'd concentrate more on telling me what I did wrong. He probably didn't brag on me as much as he should have, but I think it helped me.

— Boyd Rice

Boyd and Halee raised three children in Spearman — son Tatum (born 1985), son Tarin (born 1989), and daughter Trea (born 1997). Both sons followed their father into professional competition. Tarin Rice became the 2012 NCHA Futurity Co-Champion and was later inducted into the NCHA Hall of Fame as the youngest open rider ever so honored at the time. Tatum Rice is an active NCHA professional with career earnings exceeding $2.5 million. The Rice family represents three consecutive generations of NCHA Hall of Famers.

Oh Cay N Short — "Coyote"

The Same Horse — Two Championships — Seven Years Apart

In the history of the National Reined Cow Horse Association, no horse-and-rider combination had ever won both the Snaffle Bit Futurity — the sport's premier three-year-old championship — and the World's Greatest Horseman. Boyd Rice and Oh Cay N Short changed that, across a seven-year span that included the horse's entire competitive career, a change of ownership, and one extraordinary Christmas gift.

The 2007 Snaffle Bit Futurity win was the culmination of Boyd's sixth attempt at the event. Riding Oh Cay N Short in the finals, he scored 217 in the herd work, 213.5 in the reining, and a thrilling 222.5 going down the fence. Most cows run down the arena wall; Boyd's cow broke down the center of the arena, forcing a turn with a much higher degree of difficulty. He held on and delivered the winning performance.

Originally owned by Kevin and Sydney Knight, Oh Cay N Short was gifted to Boyd as a Christmas present in 2012 — the same year Tarin co-won the NCHA Futurity and a year when the Rice family seemed to be everywhere. Two years later, Coyote and Boyd returned to the NRCHA World's Greatest Horseman and won it by half a point in the final fence run. "He's so seasoned, and so good in all four events," Boyd said. "He's a special horse."

The 2014 NCHA World Finals result deserves special attention. Boyd showed CR Sun Reys to win the Mercuria NCHA World Finals — and set an all-time record composite score of 924 points across four rounds, winning each go-round with scores of 231, 229, 231, and 233. Winning all four rounds of the World Finals is an accomplishment of extraordinary consistency. The 924 composite was the highest in the event's history at that point.

Boyd's unique position in the performance horse world comes from his willingness — and ability — to cross disciplines. When Dick Pieper suggested he try the World's Greatest Horseman competition, Boyd added roping and reining to his cutting skills, built his NRCHA competitive resume from scratch, and within a few years was winning at the highest level of that sport too. He uses his futurity colts to do real ranch work — gathering cattle in open pastures — as part of letting horses be horses rather than show horses.
