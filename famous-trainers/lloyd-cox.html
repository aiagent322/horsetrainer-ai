The Story — Not Born Into It

High School Discovery, Oklahoma Red Dirt, and a Goal Set Without a Ceiling

Lloyd Cox was not raised with a cutting horse background. He discovered the sport in high school, grew to appreciate it, and decided that this was what he wanted to do with his life. That is the complete origin story of the winningest cutting horse rider in history. No family dynasty, no ranch upbringing, no built-in access to the world he would eventually come to dominate. Just a high school kid in Oklahoma who watched cutting horses and decided he wanted to be a trainer.

He posted his first check to EquiStat in 1985 — $2,855, the beginning of what would become an unbroken streak of annual earnings that has never stopped. Every year since 1985, Lloyd Cox has earned money on cutting horses. Every year for four decades, he has been in the competition arena. He was inducted into the NCHA Rider Hall of Fame in 2000, was the NCHA's leading money earner in 2003 ($714,583) and again in 2010 and 2012, and in 2021 became the first rider in Western performance horse history to cross the $10 million mark in total earnings.

I hadn't thought about it ever happening. I don't know what words to put with it. I'm happy, proud, humbled. I look back and it was very difficult — but I was never aiming for ten million.

— Lloyd Cox, on crossing $10 million in career earnings (2021)

He is, in the description of nearly everyone who knows him, "an unassuming cowboy." His training philosophy, asked of him in interviews, is two words: "Work hard." He is known throughout the sport as one of the nicest people in cutting — a man who shows up to help peers during their runs, who turns back cattle for fellow competitors, who handles acclaim with the same equanimity he applies to a bad day in the pen. The $10 million happened the way it did: consistently, over decades, one good horse at a time.

The Mares That Built a Career

Nurse Rey, Royal Serena Belle, Stylish Play Lena — The Thread Through Everything

One of the most remarkable features of Lloyd Cox's career is the pattern of the mares. Time and again, he rode a mare to significant competition results — and then the mare became one of the sport's great producers, and Cox rode her famous offspring too. Three in particular stand as the pillars of a career built on recognizing and developing exceptional horses regardless of gender.

Nurse Rey
Dam of Dual Rey — $44M Sire · Cox LTE $65,586
"The most cow-smart horse I ever rode in my life. I don't think I'll ever ride another horse that was that smart about a cow." Characterized by a nose-to-the-ground style. Her son: Dual Rey — one of the most influential cutting sires in history ($44M offspring earnings). Cox rode Dual Rey too.

Stylish Play Lena
$289,624 LTE · Cutting's #2 Dam All-Time
Cox showed her to $264,474 and the 2003 NCHA Classic Challenge title. She became cutting's #2 all-time leading dam with $1.9M+ in progeny earnings — including Hottish (NCHA Derby Champion with Cox), Halreycious, Reydiculous. Cox trained and rode three of her major offspring.

The pattern is not coincidence — it reflects something fundamental about how Cox operates. He identifies exceptional horses, develops them fully, and builds relationships with bloodlines over time rather than chasing individual transactions. When owners sent Hottish to Cox as a three-year-old, owner Dustin Adams explained simply: "Lloyd had always showed up to help me inside and out of the arena. I knew he was good with studs and rode the mother and knew the family, and it was a good fit." That is the Lloyd Cox business model.

2010 NCHA Futurity — The One That Mattered Most

One Time Royalty, Score 230 — From Cut-Off to Champion

The most memorable moment of Lloyd Cox's career — his own answer, consistently — was winning the 2010 NCHA Futurity on One Time Royalty. What makes the story memorable is not just the score; it's what came before it. The night before the finals, in the semi-finals, One Time Royalty had barely survived with a cut-off score of 215 — the last score that made the cut. Nobody watching thought this horse would win the next day.

Then came the finals run. One Time Royalty and Cox entered the herd, and what unfolded was a performance that scored 230 — a record at the time — and won the championship by more than any margin in recent memory. From nearly failing to qualify to posting the highest final score in memory, in a single night. The horse had a big stop, was very broke and sensitive — "After he'd come back from the breeding barn he'd be real happy," Cox recalled with characteristic warmth. "He'd always buck, and it wasn't a mean buck, he was just feeling good. When he was dialed-in he was an exceptional individual." One Time Royalty earned $447,383 in his career and was inducted into the NCHA Hall of Fame in 2013.

The son of One Time Pepto, One Time Royalty was out of Royal Serena Belle — the same mare Cox had ridden to the 1996 NCHA Futurity Reserve Championship fourteen years earlier. He had finished second on the dam. He won the title on the son.

His $10 million milestone was actually a double milestone: he entered 2021 having already recorded $10 million in EquiStat (which includes multiple disciplines and additional incentives), and then at the Breeder's Invitational became the first rider to win $10 million in the NCHA alone. No other rider in any Western performance discipline had reached that mark. His biggest single year was 2019 at $798,318 — proving that the career is still climbing.
