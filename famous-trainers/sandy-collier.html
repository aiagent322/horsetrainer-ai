From Massachusetts to Santa Barbara

An English Saddle, the Emergency Room, and the Road to California Cow Horses

Sandy Collier was born in Massachusetts and raised in New England — about as far from California cattle country as a person can be raised and still end up the most important female figure in reined cow horse history. She showed horses from age six in an English saddle, competing throughout her school years in the equestrian disciplines of the East Coast. She decided college wasn't the path for her. She traveled west across Canada and, in 1972 at age nineteen, arrived in Santa Barbara, California.

At the Tajiguas Ranch in Santa Barbara, she found work that suited her: feeding, grooming, training, and shoeing horses. She also learned saddle making, tack braiding, and colt breaking — a range of skills that no riding school teaches, that you acquire only by working. With the encouragement of Doug Ingersoll, she began competing in the California Reined Cow Horse Association (CRCHA, now NRCHA) on Quarter Horses. In 1979 she moved to Santa Ynez and spent a year working for trainer Tom Shelly before going out on her own — establishing her own training business in 1980.

She credits her aptitude for reining from her English-riding background, where dressage is focused on basics. The classics transfer. A horse properly trained is a horse properly trained.

— On Collier's analytical approach

The early years of independence were not easy. She managed a full training barn while working part-time as an emergency technician for a mobile life support company — and later in a hospital emergency room. The discipline, the decisiveness, and the composure that work demands — those transferred too. In the early 1980s, she began building her name in CRCHA competition: an open bridle championship, a year-end open hackamore championship, a Snaffle Bit Futurity open finals placing, and a ladies division reserve championship. The title itself was still ahead. But the foundation was set.

1993 — Miss Rey Dry and History Made

Finding "The One" at the Tejon Ranch — A Title That Stood Alone for Decades

1993 NRCHA World Championship Snaffle Bit Futurity Open
Sandy Collier — First and Only Woman to Win
Miss Rey Dry  ·  Dry Doc × Starlita Seguin × Rey Jay
1993
A record that stood alone for decades · NRCHA's most prestigious open title
"There was one mare who looked a little Thoroughbred-y. I think it reminded me of the old days, because that's what I showed when I did the three-day event horses. There was something about the way she moved that made me think she was 'the one.'"

In 1992, clients David and Paula Hunsicker asked Sandy Collier to find them a Snaffle Bit Futurity prospect. She went to the Tejon Ranch — where she met future NRCHA Hall of Famer Doug Williamson — and found a mare among the horses there who caught her eye. Not because the mare fit the obvious profile of a reined cow horse. Because of the way she moved. Something Thoroughbred-y about her, something reminiscent of the three-day event horses Collier had ridden years ago in a different discipline entirely. That intuition — born from a life across multiple equestrian worlds — identified Miss Rey Dry.

Collier bought the mare and developed her through the 1992 season. In 1991, she had already won the herd work preliminaries at the Snaffle Bit Futurity open division on Skip To My Joye — close but not the title. Now, with Miss Rey Dry at the peak of her form, Collier returned to Reno. The 1993 NRCHA Snaffle Bit Futurity Open Championship went to Sandy Collier and Miss Rey Dry. It was the first time in the history of the event that a woman had won the open division — a division that requires competing head-to-head against the best professional trainers in the sport, with no separate women's division, no handicap, no accommodation. The same arena, the same cattle, the same judges.

The record stood alone for decades. Sandy Collier's website notes that it has since been matched — Sarah Dawson later became the second woman to win the Snaffle Bit Futurity — but for thirty years, Collier held that distinction entirely by herself.

Beyond the wins, Collier's place in the sport is also built on her judging, teaching, and advocacy. She is an NRCHA AAA Judge — the highest certification the association offers — and an AQHA judge. She has judged all NRCHA major events, the AQHA World Show (multiple times), the World's Greatest Horseman, the Art of the Cowgirl's World's Greatest Horsewoman, and the prestigious Road to the Horse. The range of events she has judged at the highest level reflects the breadth of her knowledge and the trust the equestrian community places in her eye and her judgment.

Horse & Rider magazine named her one of the "Top 50 Riders of All Time in All Disciplines" — a list that spans English, Western, racing, polo, and every performance discipline in the horse world. She earned that recognition not for a single historic win but for a career of sustained excellence across multiple disciplines, from the English paddocks of Massachusetts to the cow pens of California.

Books, Clinics & Legacy

Teaching the Next Generation — The Career That Never Stopped Building

Sandy Collier's influence extends well beyond competition. She is an international clinician who has shared her analytical approach to cow horse training across the United States and abroad. She is the co-author of a book on horsemanship, and has published instructional videos for riders at all levels. More recently, she and Barbra Schulte teamed up to create BeUnstoppable — a program combining high-performance horse training clinics with the mental skills for peak performance. They also host women's retreats that pair riding instruction with personal development.

She has received the Monty Roberts Lifetime Achievement Award from the Art of the Cowgirl program — recognition of a career built on breaking barriers, sharing knowledge, and advancing the sport for women and men alike. The analytical philosophy that defines her approach — crediting her English and dressage background for teaching her the importance of basics and fundamentals — has shaped how she teaches and how she evaluates horses as a judge. "Collier's analytical approach to training and her consistency in the show pen are esteemed by everyone who knows her."
