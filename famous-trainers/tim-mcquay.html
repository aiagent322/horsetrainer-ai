Early Life & Career Start

Minnesota Roots, First NRHA Money in Columbus, Ohio — 1978

Tim McQuay grew up in Minnesota and came to reining horses through the Midwest horse world of the 1970s — a time when the NRHA was a young, regional organization and the Futurity was still held in Columbus, Ohio. He got started in reining in the early 1970s, and in 1978 he earned his first National Reining Horse Association money at the NRHA Futurity in Columbus: a 13th-place finish aboard Band Of Glo, earning a little over eleven hundred dollars.

It was an unremarkable result on paper. What happened in the thirty-plus years that followed is anything but. From that $1,100 first check in Columbus, Tim McQuay built the most dominant financial career in the history of competitive reining — becoming the first $2 Million Dollar rider in NRHA history, then the first $3 Million Dollar rider, and spending eleven consecutive years as the NRHA's All-Time Leading Money Earner. He won prize money on 229 different horses across his career. He won every major NRHA sanctioned event: Futurity, Derby, National Reining Breeders Classic, SuperStakes. He qualified for the NRHA Futurity finals every year but one since 1978.

I am proud that I have the ability to transform a gangly yearling into an elegant reining horse. I believe I have been extremely lucky to get where I am today — and to still be able to do what I love.

— Tim McQuay

He and his wife Colleen — a hunter-jumper specialist who became a founding board member of the United States Hunter Jumper Association — built McQuay Stables into one of the most recognized names in the sport. In the early years they showed extensively at both AQHA and USEF events. By the mid-1980s they were able to specialize: Tim in reiners, Colleen in hunters and jumpers.

Hollywood Dun It & the Move to Texas

The Foundation Stallion — and the Decision That Changed Everything

In 1986, Tim McQuay was given the opportunity to train and show a dun stallion named Hollywood Dun It — by Hollywood Jac 86, out of Blossom Berry. The horse was owned by Cliff and Gwendy Steif. That year, with McQuay in the saddle, Hollywood Dun It finished as Reserve Champion at the NRHA Futurity Open. Tim believed in the horse completely. In 1987, he and Colleen purchased Hollywood Dun It from the Steifs.

What followed was a championship record that confirmed the purchase as one of the best decisions in reining history. Tim and Hollywood Dun It won the 1987 NRHA Derby Open Championship and the 1987 NRHA SuperStakes Championship — two of the three major titles in American reining in the same year. And beyond his show career, it was what Hollywood Dun It could produce in the breeding shed that would transform McQuay Stables into a dynasty. His foals' earnings eventually exceeded $7 million, making him an NRHA Hall of Fame inductee and one of the most influential breeding stallions in the sport's history.

It was Hollywood Dun It's breeding career that drove the most consequential decision of the McQuay family's professional life: in 1989, they moved from Minnesota to Tioga, Texas. Warmer weather and easier access to breeding clientele were essential for a stallion at that level, and Tioga — a small north Texas town — became the permanent home of McQuay Stables. It has remained a focal point of the reining world ever since.

Championship Record

Every Major Title, 229 Horses, and Records That Redefined the Sport

The breadth of Tim McQuay's competitive record is staggering in its consistency across decades. He became the NRHA's first $1 Million Dollar Rider in 1997 (the second rider in history to cross that mark), its first $2 Million Dollar Rider in 2005, and its first $3 Million Dollar Rider in 2012. Through all of it, he maintained his position as the NRHA's All-Time Leading Money Earner for eleven straight years — a dominance of the financial record that no other trainer has approached.

His two NRHA Futurity Championship wins — on Mr Melody Jac in 1988 and a second title in 2008 at age 56 — are by his own account his proudest career moments. The 2008 win, achieved while qualifying three separate horses for the Futurity finals in the same year, demonstrated an extraordinary depth of horsemanship that most trainers could not replicate at half his age. In 2001, he returned from a life-threatening illness mid-season and still made the NRHA Futurity Finals — a testament to the resilience and commitment that defined his entire career.

He is also a five-time All-American Quarter Horse Congress Futurity Champion, a four-time Southwest Reining Horse Association Futurity Champion, a two-time AQHA World Champion, and a multiple NRHA World and Reserve World Champion.

The Stallion Legacy

Hollywood Dun It, Colonels Smoking Gun, and the McQuay Breeding Dynasty

McQuay Stables became synonymous not just with competitive dominance but with breeding excellence. The stallions that Tim and Colleen brought to Tioga shaped the genetic direction of competitive reining for a generation. Two of them — Hollywood Dun It and Colonels Smoking Gun — are buried at McQuay Stables, the final resting place of both NRHA Hall of Fame sires.

Hollywood Dun It
NRHA & AQHA Hall of Fame · $7M+ sire earnings
By Hollywood Jac 86. 1987 NRHA Derby Champion and SuperStakes Champion with McQuay. Purchased by McQuay in 1987. His foals' earnings exceeded $7 million. Leading offspring include Hollywoodstinseltown ($178K), Reminic N Dunit ($169K), Matt Dillion Dun It ($169K), Hollywood Vintage — 1999 NRHA Futurity Champion. Buried at McQuay Stables.
Mr Melody Jac
NRHA Futurity Champion 1988 · Hollywood Jac 86 son
Ridden by Tim McQuay to the 1988 NRHA Futurity Open Championship — one of his two proudest career moments. By Hollywood Jac 86. NRHA lifetime earnings $112,239. The "Mr Melody Jac" bloodline continued in Yellow Jersey (out of Ms Clara Melody, a Mr Melody Jac daughter), who Mandy McCutcheon rode to WEG bronze in 2014.
Colonels Smoking Gun
NRHA Hall of Fame · $5M+ Sire · "Gunner"
NRHA $5 Million Sire and Hall of Fame inductee. One of the most influential reining sires in history. Died tragically in July 2013 — a loss that McQuay described as leaving an indelible mark on the stables and the entire industry. Buried at McQuay Stables alongside Hollywood Dun It.
Hollywoodstinseltown
Hollywood Dun It son · WEG 2010 Gold Medal
Son of Hollywood Dun It. Carried Tim McQuay to Team Gold at the 2010 World Equestrian Games in Lexington, Kentucky. NRHA earnings $178,156. His first foal crop included Nuthin But Hollywood, ridden by Tim to the 2012 Tulsa Developing Horse Futurity Championship.
The Reining First Family

Three Generations of NRHA Million Dollar Riders

Tim McQuay's legacy extends beyond his own competitive record into one of the most remarkable family dynasties in the history of the sport. His daughter Mandy McQuay married Tom McCutcheon, himself an elite reiner. Together, their family has produced three NRHA Million Dollar Riders across two generations — and a third generation in their children Cade and Carlee is already making history.

The 2014 World Equestrian Games brought a particularly poignant family moment: Tim owned Yellow Jersey — a stallion he had purchased in late 2013 — and he and Colleen decided that Mandy was the rider for the Games. Mandy rode Yellow Jersey to a 227 in the Individual Medal Finals in Normandy, France, earning the Individual Bronze Medal and becoming the first female in history to earn an Individual Medal in Reining at the WEG. A horse Tim owned, ridden by his daughter, making history on the world's biggest equestrian stage. The maternal grandsire of Yellow Jersey was Mr Melody Jac — the horse Tim had ridden to the 1988 NRHA Futurity Championship, his first proudest moment.
