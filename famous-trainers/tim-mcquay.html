Background

Tim McQuay

Tim McQuay got started in reining in the 1970s. He moved his family from Minnesota to Tioga, Texas in 1989. He earned his first NRHA paycheck in 1978 at the NRHA Futurity when it was still held in Columbus, Ohio, riding Band Of Glo to a 13th-place finish and earning just over eleven hundred dollars. He qualified for the NRHA Futurity finals every year but one since that first check. He has earned reining prize money on 229 different horses. His son-in-law is Tom McCutcheon, also a prominent reining trainer.

Career

Competitive Record

McQuay was inducted into the NRHA Hall of Fame in 2000. He became the first $2 Million Dollar Rider in NRHA history and was the NRHA's All-Time Leading Money Earner for 11 years. He has won every major NRHA sanctioned event: the NRHA Futurity, the National Reining Breeders Classic, the NRHA Derby, and the NRHA Superstakes.

In 2001, despite being sidelined mid-year with a life-threatening illness, he was an NRHA Futurity finalist on Smart Miss Marker (owned by Kanjiro Tokita). In 2002 he finished Reserve Champion of the NRHA Derby on Kid Whiz (owned by Brian Fix) and was a member of the Gold Medal-winning US Team at the Bayer/USET CRIO Nation's Cup in Gladstone, New Jersey on Okie Dun Did It (owned by Monica Hicks). In 2003 he won the NRHA Derby Open Championship on RR Star (owned by Richard and Rose Lundin) and the AQHA Senior Reining World Championship on Custom Red Berry (a roan mare, owned by Stone Canyon Ranch). In 2005 he won a silver medal at the FEI Masters Reining in Italy. At the 2006 World Equestrian Games he won the Individual Silver Medal and helped the US secure Team Gold. One of the horses he led to a major title is Shining Spark, whom he rode to win the 1993 AQHA Junior Reining World Championship with a score of 227.5. Shining Spark became an NRHA Hall of Fame inductee and a $4 Million Dollar Sire.
