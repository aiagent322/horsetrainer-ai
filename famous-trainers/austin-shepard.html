Early Life — Alabama Roots

Summerdale, Alabama — Growing Up Cutting, Son of a Hall of Famer

Austin Shepard grew up in the cutting horse business as the son of Sam Shepard — an NCHA Hall of Fame rider and trainer who operated out of Alabama and whose influence on his son's career was total. Austin's parents split when he was young, and he and his mother moved into the city. He was very involved in sports during his school years, but in the summers he would go ride horses with his dad. The fact that he wasn't around the horses all the time, he later said, only increased his interest when he was there. You want what you can't always have.

He has been an active NCHA member since 1985 — the first entry in his career records is a fifty-dollar check from a youth event at the Heart of Dixie Cutting Horse Association, the same year. The sport ran in the blood and he never considered anything else. He was inducted into the NCHA Riders Hall of Fame in 2005. His father Sam had received the NCHA Zane Schulte Award in 2009, recognizing his integrity, service, and contributions to the sport. Austin received the same award in 2022 — the only father and son to share that honor.

I love training a good horse and I love the relationship between a cow and a horse. It's what drives me.

— Austin Shepard

He has spent his career at The Circle S — his ranch in Summerdale, Alabama, about forty miles southeast of Mobile, near the Gulf Coast. It is one of the few elite cutting operations in the deep South, and Shepard has made it one of the most respected facilities in the sport. His wife Stacy, inducted into the NCHA Non-Pro Riders Hall of Fame in 2009, has been his partner throughout. Their son Cade is now the youngest NCHA Non-Pro Hall of Fame inductee ever. Their daughter Caylee also rides. The Shepards are a cutting horse family in the deepest sense — three generations, four Hall of Fame inductees, one ranch in Alabama.

A Cutting Dynasty

Three Generations — Four NCHA Hall of Fame Inductees from One Family

The Shepard family is the most decorated cutting horse family dynasty in terms of combined Hall of Fame recognition. No other family in the sport can claim Hall of Fame inductees across three consecutive generations, in both the Open and Non-Pro divisions.

The 2007 NCHA Futurity — High Brow CD

Score 226, Won by 8.5 Points — and Nearly Went to Someone Else

The story of High Brow CD almost never happened. Austin Shepard was at the John Justin Sale Arena during the 2005 NCHA Futurity Sales — not looking for the colt but working another horse in the practice pen — when owner Gary Noble asked for his opinion. "I told him that I loved the mare (High Brow CD's dam, Sweet Little CD) and we should look at the baby," Shepard recalled. Noble bought the colt for $63,000. "If I hadn't gone to work another horse in the practice pen, Clint Allen could have had that horse. I stood right beside Clint and he was bidding against me."

Two years later, at the 2007 NCHA Futurity, Shepard and High Brow CD entered the second set of cattle and upped the ante late in the run. Their 226-point score topped the field by eight and a half points — a commanding win on a horse that the right person had been in exactly the right place to identify. "I was just trying to be smooth on my second cow, but it wouldn't let me off and I kept having to be more aggressive," Shepard said. "I didn't think it was ever going to turn away. My horse was out of air." High Brow CD went on to become the highest-earning horse Shepard ever rode — $543,161 in lifetime NCHA earnings.

The Year of the Century — 2017

World Champion, Futurity Champion, Derby Champion — All in One Season

In 2017, Austin Shepard had one of the greatest single seasons in the history of cutting horse competition. First, he defended his position throughout the NCHA Open World Championship with Deluxe Checks — scoring 233 in two go-rounds at the World Finals and setting a record for the most money won at World Finals in history ($146,977). He was the 2017 NCHA Open World Champion. Then, one week later, he won the NCHA Futurity Open Championship on Dual Reyish.

The Dual Reyish story is one of the sport's most memorable. The horse was owned by Billy Wolf of Whitesboro, Texas — who had only purchased the horse on the advice of his agent Don Ham that May. And here is the detail that makes the story: when Dual Reyish won the 2017 NCHA Futurity Open Championship, it was the first time Billy Wolf had ever met the horse. It was also the first time he had met Austin Shepard. Their first interaction was the night before the final. The next day, their horse won the sport's biggest title.

Deluxe Checks earned $224,334. All three horses ridden to career-defining performances in the same season. That year alone pushed Austin Shepard from approximately $7 million to more than $8 million in career earnings, cementing his status as one of the greatest cutting trainers in the sport's history.

Kittennish — "The Queen"

The Best Horse He's Ever Ridden — A Michael Jordan Personality

Horse of a Lifetime
Kittennish
2019 Mare · Hottish × Show Biz Kitty · 2023 NCHA Open Horse of the Year · 2024 Run For A Million Champion · $549K+ NCHA Earnings
"She's the best horse I've ever ridden in my life. I'd love to take all the credit but she just wants this. She's a Michael Jordan-type personality, and she came that way."

In the modern era of Shepard's career, one mare has stood above all others: Kittennish. By Hottish out of Show Biz Kitty (High Brow Cat), the sorrel mare arrived in Shepard's program with a competitive instinct so pronounced that Shepard compares her to the most singularly driven competitor in basketball history. "I mean, she's a Michael Jordan-type personality, and she came that way," he told Quarter Horse News after winning the 2024 Run for a Million. "I'd love to take all the credit but she just wants this."

Kittennish's resume across 2023 and 2024 is staggering: 2022 NCHA Futurity Open Reserve Champion; 2023 NCHA Open Horse of the Year; 2023 Augusta Futurity 4-Year-Old Open Champion; 2023 Southern Cutting Futurity 4-Year-Old Open Champion; 2023 Brazos Bash 4-Year-Old Open Champion; 2023 Pacific Coast Cutting Futurity 4-Year-Old Open Champion; 2024 Run for a Million Cutting Champion ($50,000). Each year the mare adds to a résumé that has made her one of the most celebrated cutting horses of her generation — and Shepard has been in the saddle for every step of it.
