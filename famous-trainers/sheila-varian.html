Background

Sheila Varian

Sheila Varian was born on August 8, 1937, in Santa Maria, California, and grew up in Halcyon, California. She died on March 6, 2016, in Arroyo Grande, California, at age 78, after a battle with ovarian cancer diagnosed in 2013. Her grandfather was John Osborne Varian; her uncles were Russell and Sigurd Varian, inventors of the klystron tube. She was inspired as a child by books by Marguerite Henry and the Black Stallion series by Walter Farley, which made her interested in Arabian horses. Her first horsemanship mentor was Mary "Sid" Spencer, a local rancher and Morgan horse breeder who also introduced her to the vaquero (Californio) tradition of western riding. Her second mentor was Tom Dorrance, who taught her an expanded version of the vaquero soft approach, which she adapted to fit the Arabian horse's intelligent, sensitive temperament. She graduated from Cal Poly San Luis Obispo and taught physical education at Arroyo Grande High School for three years before going full-time at the ranch in 1963.

In 1954, with her parents' help, she launched Varian Arabians on the family's ranch near Arroyo Grande, California. In 1961 she was among the first Americans to import purebred Arabian horses from Poland — purchasing three mares (*Naganka, *Bachantka, *Ostroga) at the height of communist occupation. In October 1961 she won the Open Reined Cow Horse Championship at the Cow Palace in San Francisco on her Arabian mare Ronteza — the first amateur, first woman, and first Arabian horse to win that title. In 1962 she showed the stallion Bay-Abi (Errabi × Angyl) to the US National Champion Stallion title. In 1969 she trained and showed Bay Event (Bay-Abi × Ronteza) to Open Reined Cow Horse Championships throughout California; Bay Event was twice named US National Champion Arabian Stock Horse, unanimously, in 1971 and 1976. In 1981 she was invited to judge the Polish National Championship. In 1983 she and Don Severa syndicated Huckleberry Bey++ for $3 million.

Varian Arabians grew to a 230-acre farm with 150 horses. By 2016 she had registered 1,180 horses. Eighty-four percent of Arabian show horses winning at that time carried Varian blood. Her foundation stallion line ran six generations: Bay-Abi++ through Bay El Bey++, Huckleberry Bey++, Desperado V and Maclintock V. Her foundation mare lines reached nine generations. She was inducted into the National Cowgirl Hall of Fame in 2003. In 2015 she arranged with the California Rangeland Trust to protect the ranch from development after her death.
