Early Life

Long Beach, California — A Pony Named Dizzy and a Life Redirected

Matt Mills was born and raised in Long Beach, California — about as far from a cattle ranch as you can get in the American West. His introduction to horses came at age eight via a pony named Dizzy at the Lakewood Equestrian Center in Long Beach. That first lesson in a small round pen opened a door he has never closed.

As a youth rider, Mills built a remarkable competitive record throughout California in multiple western disciplines — western pleasure, horsemanship, and trail. He was not merely competent across these disciplines; he earned multiple championships, developing the cross-discipline feel and competitive instinct that would later serve him well when the fastest, most demanding form of western riding caught his attention.

There's ups and downs, and if you just continue to stay the course — of course, you've got to have the horses and all that good stuff that goes with it — but if you stay the course and keep working your butt off, there's nothing you can't get to.

— Matt Mills

In 1996 he made the pivotal decision: reining. He was not yet a professional, and the transition was not immediate — it came through the classic path of learning from someone better. In 1997, the day after high school graduation, he drove to Arizona for what was supposed to be only a summer internship. It lasted five years and launched a career.

The Foundation

Dell Hendricks, the NRHA Derby, and Eyes Wide Open

The summer internship was with Dell Hendricks — himself an NRHA earner of nearly $2 million, a high-level professional with a serious program and a serious competitive calendar. The first week Mills was there, Hendricks went to the NRHA Derby and nearly won it with Randy Paul on a horse named Hangten Peppy. "My eyes were wide open," Mills recalled. The exposure was immediate and total: this was the level he wanted to reach.

He spent approximately five years with Hendricks, learning the profession from the inside — how to start futurity horses, how to develop a training program, how to manage clients and compete at the highest level while running a daily operation. He joined NRHA as a youth member in 1997 and moved into his career as an NRHA Professional in 2003. His first major win came at the 2000 NRHA Futurity, taking home the Open Level 3 Reserve title on Dunnits Delight — a strong opening statement before he even had his professional card.

When he eventually went out on his own, he established his training operation in Scottsdale, Arizona — building a program that would attract quality horses and loyal owners, several of whom, including Mark and Janice Dickson, Tim and Mary Beth Ruckman, and George and Cam Ranzau, stayed with him for decades.

The Career-Defining Horse

Easy Otie Whiz — 231.5 and a World Gold Medal

Every trainer, in retrospect, has one horse who changed the arc of their career. For Matt Mills, that horse was Easy Otie Whiz — a 1999 stallion by Topsail Whiz out of Miss Poco Easy who stood at Out West Stallion Station. "Easy Otie Whiz really improved the trajectory of my career," Mills has said. "I made a lot of open level 4 finals on him and also attained my all-time highest score under five judges."

That score came at the 2006 United States Equestrian Federation (USEF) Open Reining Championship in Lexington, Kentucky: 231.5. Under five judges, with a career-high run that was as good as anything Mills had ever done in the arena. The score qualified him for Team USA — and what Team USA did next became the defining moment of his competitive life.

At the 2006 World Equestrian Games in Aachen, Germany, Matt Mills rode Easy Otie Whiz as part of the United States reining team that won the gold medal. The WEG is the Olympics of equestrian sport — the FEI's premier multi-discipline world championship, held every four years. A gold medal there, as part of a team representing the entire country in a discipline that was only accepted into FEI competition in 2000, is about as high as reining competition goes. "Winning the gold medal for Team USA in 2006 on Easy Otie Whiz was a standout for me and my whole family," he has said. "They say everyone has that one breakout horse and 'Easy' was definitely that horse for me. I will always have a place for that horse in my heart — he was a special individual."

Television & Public Profile

The Last Cowboy, Yellowstone, and Reining's Biggest Mainstream Moment

Matt Mills is, by a significant margin, the most publicly visible reining trainer of his generation — a distinction that owes much to his presence in two of the most culturally prominent horse-related television productions of the modern era. Both emerged from the orbit of Taylor Sheridan, the creator and showrunner of Yellowstone, and both took the relatively niche world of professional reining to audiences who had never heard of an NRHA Futurity.

The Last Cowboy
CMT / Paramount Network · 2019–present
Taylor Sheridan's documentary-competition series chronicling elite horse reining competition and the Run for a Million — a $1 million reining event in Las Vegas. Mills has been a recurring cast member across multiple seasons, bringing his career, his horses, and his training philosophy to mainstream audiences. Currently in Season 6.
Yellowstone
Paramount Network
The most-watched scripted series in American cable television history. Mills appeared in the Yellowstone universe — filmed at Taylor Sheridan's Bosque Ranch in Weatherford, Texas, where Mills now bases his operation — bringing his horsemanship to one of the defining pop-culture cowboy stories of the 2020s.
Run for a Million
Taylor Sheridan production · Las Vegas
Created by Sheridan in 2019 — a $1 million reining competition at the South Point Arena in Las Vegas. Mills has competed and served as guest commentator. The event puts reining on a stage that matches the sport's athleticism with production values that reach beyond the traditional horse show audience.

Mills's public presence extends beyond television. He maintains an active YouTube channel — Matt Mills Reining — where he produces training content, behind-the-scenes barn videos, and educational material for riders at every level. His Facebook page has more than 140,000 followers, a number that reflects an online community built not just on his competitive record but on his willingness to share the daily reality of professional reining with people who may never enter an NRHA arena themselves.

I'm going out in the arena as an individual, but this is a team sport. I can't stress enough how important the horses, owners, my family, and everyone on my team is to me. My team is really the key to my success.

— Matt Mills on reaching NRHA Million Dollar status

He competed at Road to the Horse 2022 — the prestigious colt-starting competition at the Kentucky Horse Park — expanding his competitive profile into yet another discipline-crossing arena. He is headquartered at Taylor Sheridan's Bosque Ranch in Weatherford, Texas, training and showing futurity and derby horses while coaching non-pro and youth riders. He is also in demand as a clinician in the United States, Europe, and Mexico.
