In Memoriam

A Sudden Loss at the Place He Loved Most

Robert Charles "Bob" Avila died unexpectedly on November 9, 2024, at the age of 72 — at the American Quarter Horse Association World Championship Show in Oklahoma City. He had been there, as he had been every year for as long as anyone could remember, to help his wife Dana and other amateur riders compete. He died doing what he loved, where he loved doing it. "I don't know if he's ever missed a World Show," said Todd Bergen, one of the many accomplished trainers who came up under him. "The World Show is something that he looked forward to every year."

The western performance horse community mourned deeply. Avila had been one of its most accomplished, most versatile, and most generous figures — a man who could win in the halter pen, the pleasure pen, the reining pen, and the cutting pen, and who spent decades ensuring that the people who worked for him had the opportunities to win in those same pens. He was survived by his wife Dana and son BJ Avila.

Horses have given me everything I have. Quarter Horses have helped make me what I am. They have been my life's work, and I owe them everything.

— Bob Avila, 2020
Early Life

Born Into Horses — Redwood City, California, 1951

Robert Charles "Bob" Avila was born in November 1951 in Redwood City, California, into a family built around horses. His father, Don Avila, was a rodeo cowboy and horse trainer. His mother, Pat Avila, gave riding lessons and worked at a western store. The household revolved around the American Quarter Horse Association — both parents were actively involved in AQHA competition, and horses were as constant a presence in the Avila home as furniture.

He showed his first horse at age five, riding everything from his father's rope horses to his mother's pleasure horses — absorbing, without any formal curriculum, the full breadth of what a horse could do and what a horseman needed to know across disciplines. That early exposure to everything from halter to performance would define his entire career: he would become, in the words of those who knew him best, the last great all-around horseman.

After high school, he left home to establish his own path. That path inevitably returned to horses. At age twenty, Bob Avila went to work for Tony Amaral — an NRCHA Hall of Fame member and one of the founding figures of the National Reined Cow Horse Association. Amaral's operation gave Avila the competitive reined cow horse foundation that would drive the most decorated chapter of his career.

All-Around Mastery

The Last Man Who Could Win Everything

What made Bob Avila genuinely singular was the breadth of his accomplishment. In an era of increasing specialization — when the best reining trainers focused exclusively on reining, the best cutters on cutting, the best pleasure trainers on pleasure — Avila won them all. His 37 World Champion and Reserve World Champion titles ranged from halter horses to performance horses, from Western riding and trail to rope horses, from reining to working cow horse. NRCHA judge Jim Spence put it in the clearest possible terms:

"His true love was AQHA and in my mind he was probably the last great all-around horseman. He could pick out and fit a halter horse and make it a world champion. He could pick out and fit a pleasure horse and make it a champion and he could do that with a Western riding horse, a trail horse, a rope horse, reiner, cow horse and a cutter. At those AQHA shows, when I first started going, he was in all those classes and winning them. That's unheard of. Nobody does that anymore. I used to say he could train a horse to stack hay if he wanted to."

— Jim Spence, reined cow horse trainer and NRCHA judge

He trained champions in AQHA, NRHA, NRCHA, NCHA, and other associations — horses including Major Bonanza, Major Investment, The Major Leaguer, Mist N Smoke, Smoke Em Okie, Wright On, Smart Lil Calboy, Smart Zanolena, and Chics Magic Potion. Each of those names represents a different horse, a different discipline context, a different genetic line — and in each case, a champion produced by the same pair of hands and the same horsemanship philosophy.

Championship Record

The Snaffle Bit, the World's Greatest Horseman, and Records That Stand Alone

At the apex of reined cow horse competition stand two titles: the NRCHA Snaffle Bit Futurity — a three-phase event for three-year-old horses combining reining, cutting, and fence work — and the World's Greatest Horseman. Bob Avila won both. Multiple times. His record at the Snaffle Bit Futurity — three victories — puts him in the rarest of company: only one other horseman has won it more than twice. His record at the Stallion Stakes — five victories — stands alone.

He is one of only two people to have won both the $100,000 NRCHA Open Futurity and the $100,000 NRHA Open Futurity — an achievement that underscores not just his skill but his range. His NRHA career earnings reached $381,339. His NRCHA earnings reached $1,113,022. Total career earnings approached $1.5 million — an EquiStat Elite $1 Million Rider and an NRCHA Million Dollar Rider. He won the Magnificent 7 Reined Cow Horse competition three times. He was inducted into the NRCHA Hall of Fame in 2006, the AQHA Hall of Fame in 2020.

Judge, Mentor & Teacher

The Trainer's Trainer — Building the Next Generation

Bob Avila's legacy extended far beyond his own championships. He was described universally by those who knew him as "the trainer's trainer" — a man who recognized talent in young horsemen, gave them opportunities, and produced some of the most accomplished professionals in the industry today. Todd Bergen grew up in the same area of Oregon where Avila trained and worked for him nearly six years. "He put me on some good horses and I won my first couple world championships working for him. If he felt like you were up to it and you were working hard, he would give you the opportunities. He gave me a ton of opportunities and I'll never forget him for that."

Tom St. Hilaire — who built his own training facility in Yamhill, Oregon after his years with Avila — had Bob as his best man at his wedding. "There aren't going to be a lot of guys like him, because of the era he was in," St. Hilaire said. "We made some horses do things that on paper they weren't really bred to do."

Avila served as an AQHA judge for nineteen years and served on the AQHA judges committee since 2009. He was an AQHA director from Oregon for more than ten years. He was a founding member of the AQHA Professional Horsemen's Committee. In 1995 — the first year the award existed — he became the first-ever AQHA Professional Horseman of the Year. He was an NRCHA executive board member and served on the judges committee there as well.

Bob's show career has truly been one for the ages, but what really makes him stand out is the trainers that have mentored under him.

— NRCHA on Bob Avila

In his later career, Avila turned increasingly to teaching. He created training videos that set the standard for equine instructional content, produced a magazine — Ride With Bob Avila — that carried his training philosophy and advice to a broad readership, appeared with Al Dunning on the RFD-TV show The Bob and Al Show, and made regular podcast appearances sharing his career wisdom. He appeared as himself in the Yellowstone television series, Season 3, Episode 8. Even in his early seventies, he was still active, still at the World Show, still helping riders get better.
