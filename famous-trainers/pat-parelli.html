Early Life

Livermore, California — Stablehand, Rodeo Boy, and a Mule Named Thumper

Pat Parelli was born on March 17, 1954, in Livermore, California, in the rolling golden-grass hill country east of San Francisco Bay. From an early age, horses were the center of his world. As a child he worked as a stablehand at nearby horse facilities, absorbing the rhythms of equine life long before he had any formal framework for what he was learning. By age thirteen he had found his first real mentor: Freddie Ferrera, a horseman and trapper from Livermore who taught the young Parelli about natural interactions with horses and the land — a foundation that would quietly inform everything that followed.

In high school he took to the rodeo circuit, competing in bareback bronc riding with enough success to attract serious notice. His dedication earned him the Professional Rodeo Cowboys Association's Bareback Rookie of the Year title in 1972, at eighteen years old. He went on to complete a degree in agricultural education at Fresno State University — an academic grounding in both the science and the teaching of his chosen field — before returning to horses and professional competition full time.

Natural Horsemanship has become a viable and important aspect of the international horse industry and continues to expand and influence the way in which horses are trained.

— Parelli Natural Horsemanship

His rodeo success opened doors. One of them led to Troy Henry — a mentor Parelli has described as one of the greatest western horsemen in the United States. Henry took Parelli under his wing for five years, teaching him to be a horseman rather than merely a horse trainer. The distinction mattered. A trainer produces results. A horseman understands why. When Henry passed away, Parelli dedicated himself to spreading Henry's philosophy. The seed had been planted.

The Mule and the Masters

Thumper, Tom Dorrance, Ray Hunt, and the Making of a Method

In the late 1970s and early 1980s, Pat Parelli did something that announced his name to the horse world before he had a program, a brand, or even a philosophy to call his own: he took a mule named Thumper to the National Reined Cow Horse Association Snaffle Bit Futurity and won third prize. It was an audacious act of horsemanship, performed with a species the event organizers had never anticipated. The rules were reportedly updated afterward to specify horses only — a change that has since been called "The Pat Parelli Rule."

Alongside his study under Troy Henry, Parelli sought out the best horsemen working in the tradition he was drawn to. He attended clinics by Tom Dorrance, Ray Hunt, and Ronnie Willis — the three men he has named as master mentors in his development. When he met Ray Hunt at the California Livestock Symposium in 1983, Parelli described the encounter as transformative: he absorbed Hunt's philosophy of working with the horse's nature, took it seriously, and began building his own systematic expression of those principles.

In 1979, Robert M. Miller, DVM — who would become one of the founding theorists of natural horsemanship — watched a young trainer start a colt at a demonstration in Bishop, California, and said to himself: "If that guy sticks with it, he could be the world's best." That was Parelli. Miller went on to co-author a landmark three-part series with him in Western Horseman magazine between November 1983 and January 1984, titled A New Look at an Old Method. The articles introduced Parelli's approach to a national audience for the first time.

On March 1, 1982, Pat Parelli gave his very first formal seminar. He went on to celebrate the 40th anniversary of that event at the World Equestrian Center in Ocala, Florida — decades of teaching, clinic by clinic, that had grown into something no one in 1982 could have anticipated.

The Parelli Program

Love, Language, and Leadership — A System for the World

In 1982 Parelli began building his natural horsemanship program in earnest. In 1984 he began teaching clinics. In 1991 he coined the term "Natural Horsemanship" itself — a phrase that has since become the name of an entire movement and an international industry. Parelli and his second wife Linda (whom he met in Australia in 1989 and married in 1995) built the program into the most recognized horsemanship brand on the planet, headquartered first in California and then, from 1996 onward, in Pagosa Springs, Colorado.

The core principle of the program is straightforward: horsemanship is obtained naturally through communication, understanding, and psychology — not mechanics, fear, or intimidation. The structure Parelli built around that principle has three pillars: Love, Language, and Leadership. Every element of the curriculum flows from that foundation.

The program is organized into Four Savvys — four modes of working with a horse that together encompass the complete relationship:

Online
Groundwork on a lead rope — building communication and control from the ground
Liberty
Interacting without physical restraint — pure body language and true partnership
Freestyle
Riding with minimal rein contact — horse responding to seat and leg
Finesse
Advanced riding with refined cues — precision, collection, and unity

At the heart of the curriculum are the Seven Games — exercises that mirror natural horse behaviors and build the vocabulary of communication between horse and human. They are taught at Level Two of the four-level Parelli program:

Game 1
Friendly
Building rapport; demonstrating that the human and tools are not a threat
Game 2
Porcupine
Steady pressure that teaches the horse to yield and move away
Game 3
Driving
Rhythmic pressure — moving the horse without direct contact
Game 4
Yo-Yo
Back and forward movement — building respect for space and direction
Game 5
Circling
Maintaining gait and direction on a circle — responsibility and self-carriage
Game 6
Sideways
Lateral movement — suppleness and responsiveness to subtle cues
Game 7
Squeeze
Confidence through tight spaces — the horse's trust and forward impulsion
Global Reach

From Livermore to 76 Countries — One Million Students

The scale of what the Parelli program became is difficult to overstate. Over one million people worldwide have participated in the program. It has spread to 76 countries. The Savvy Club — Parelli's long-distance, online learning program — has drawn over 200,000 members. More than 250 licensed professionals around the world teach under the Parelli banner, carrying the program into every discipline and every corner of the equestrian world.

Parelli has been featured in over 1,000 magazine articles. He has presented privately for Queen Elizabeth II, President Reagan, motivational speaker Tony Robbins, Hollywood actor and horseman Gene Autry, and Tom Selleck. His work crosses disciplines — he has worked with Olympic veterans David and Karen O'Connor in the English world, and with reining star Craig Johnson in the Western arena — a deliberate demonstration that the principles of natural horsemanship belong to no single tradition.

For me, these principles are really about life. About living your life so that you're not making war with the horse, or with other people.

— Pat Parelli

In 2009, the Humane Society of the United States named Pat Parelli its Humane Horseman of the Year for his outstanding commitment to improving the welfare of America's horses — recognition from a mainstream animal welfare organization of the real-world impact of his decades of teaching.

Parelli has been headquartered in Pagosa Springs, Colorado since 1996, where he developed a landmark equestrian ranch property over nearly three decades. He and Linda Parelli divorced in 2020. His son Caton — born in 1984 with hydrocephalus, once feared to never walk or talk — overcame those early difficulties and went on to ride and show American Quarter Horses in cutting and reining, a story of determination that has moved audiences wherever Pat has told it.
