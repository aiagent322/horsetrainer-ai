The Beginning — Tapeppyoka Peppy

A 14-Year-Old Boy, His Father, and a Mare That Started Everything

Phil Rapp's cutting career began, as so many great stories do, with a gift. When Rapp was fourteen years old, his father Jerry bought him a mare named Tapeppyoka Peppy (by Doc's Oak). It was a modest purchase — a starting point rather than a statement. No one could have predicted that the bloodline carried by that one mare would, over the next five decades, produce four consecutive generations of NCHA Hall of Fame horses, all bred and raised at Rapp Ranch in Weatherford, Texas, and all shown to major championships by Phil and Mary Ann Rapp. The family involvement with this bloodline has now stretched more than 37 years and produced horses that have won over $9 million in combined earnings.

Phil Rapp began competing in 1983, earning $319.20 that first year. He moved quickly: by 1987 he had exceeded $100,000 in career earnings. He made the NCHA Non-Pro Top 15 from 1986 through 1988. He was, from the beginning, a natural — but what set him apart was the combination of competitive brilliance and an unusually deep partnership with the horses he bred himself. His wife Mary Ann became the NCHA's all-time leading non-pro money earner, with more than $4.8 million in non-pro competition. Their children Ryan and Emma have shown cutting horses as well. The Rapps are a cutting horse family, top to bottom.

Most memorable moment in cutting: The 1991 Memphis Futurity because I won the Open and Non-Pro on the same day riding Playboys Ruby. Also the 1994 NCHA Super Stakes, I won both the Open and Non-Pro on two different horses, Tap O Lena and Miss Skeeto Lena respectively. These were both great moments as my Dad was still alive and I can still remember the elation in his voice when I called him from my hotel room to tell him how I did.

— Phil Rapp

In 1994, Rapp became the first rider in NCHA history to win both the Open and Non-Pro division at the NCHA Super Stakes in the same year — the Open on Tap O Lena and the Non-Pro on Miss Skeeto Lena. It was a feat that had never been accomplished before and the emotional resonance was compounded by his father still being alive to receive the phone call. Training philosophy, distilled to its core: "Work the cow!"

A Dynasty Five Generations Deep

Tapeppyoka Peppy to Dont Stopp Believin — All Bred at Rapp Ranch

The Rapp bloodline dynasty is unprecedented in the sport of cutting. Beginning with Tapeppyoka Peppy — the mare Phil's father bought for a fourteen-year-old boy — every generation has produced an NCHA Hall of Fame horse, every one trained and competed by the Rapps themselves. Three of the five generations are NCHA Hall of Fame inductees. Together with the horses outside this bloodline that Phil and Mary Ann have shown, bred, or owned, the Rapp family has bred winners of more than $9.1 million and owned winners of more than $10.6 million — a breeding and ownership record that may never be matched in the sport.

The World Record — 234

Dont Look Twice and the Score That Still Stands

In 2011, at the Mercuria/NCHA World Series of Cutting event in Idaho, Phil Rapp and Dont Look Twice marked a score of 234 — the highest score in NCHA history. It stood as the sole world record for four years, until Matt Gaines tied it on Special Nu Baby at the El Rancho Futurity in September 2015. The 234 has not been exceeded since. It remains the co-record of the sport.

Dont Look Twice was a 2007 High Brow Cat mare out of Tapt Twice — a granddaughter of Tap O Lena, bred by Phil and Mary Ann and owned by Waco Bend Ranch, Graham, Texas. Her run that day in Idaho encapsulated everything Phil had said about her: "She's so intelligent. Her talent plus her mental strategy makes for a good horse. She could be compared to a five-tool baseball player." She earned 26 major event championships with Phil in the saddle, 8 Mercuria World Series wins (an individual horse record), the 2011 NCHA Open Horse of the Year, and the 2012 NCHA Open World Championship. Her total career earnings of $824,545 made her the all-time leading cutting horse mare in NCHA history.

The Rapp Family

Mary Ann — The All-Time Leading Non-Pro Rider — and a Family Built on Horses

Phil Rapp's accomplishments as a trainer and open competitor are inseparable from Mary Ann Rapp's parallel career as the NCHA's all-time leading non-pro money earner. With more than $4.8 million in non-pro career earnings, Mary Ann holds a record in her division comparable to what Phil has built in the open. She has shown horses from the same bloodlines Phil developed — including Tap O Lena (1997 Non-Pro World Champion), Tapt Twice (Super Stakes Non-Pro Classic Reserve), and Reyzin (2013 NCHA Non-Pro Horse of the Year) — across the entirety of the career they have built together at Rapp Ranch.

Together, Phil and Mary Ann have owned winners of more than $10.6 million and bred winners of more than $9.1 million in NCHA competition. The Rapp Ranch at 400 Smith Trail in Weatherford, Texas is both their home and the source of the bloodline that has defined Phil's competitive legacy — a place where the family of horses that has carried him since age 14 continues to grow.

Phil served as NCHA Director, NCHA Executive Committee member, NCHA Vice-President (2016), and NCHA President (2018) — contributing as a leader to the organization that shaped his career, the same way Buster Welch and the generation before him did.
