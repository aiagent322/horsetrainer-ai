The Banuelos Legacy

A Dynasty Built Across Two Generations — From Mexico to the NCHA Hall of Fame

To understand Adan Banuelos, you have to start with his father. Ascencion Banuelos left Mexico alone at thirteen years old, immigrating to California in search of a better life with nothing but the determination to find it. He started on a dairy farm milking four hundred cows twice a day. From those early mornings and long days came a discipline and a work ethic that would eventually carry him to the highest honor in the cutting horse world: Ascencion Banuelos became the first Mexican-American ever inducted into the National Cutting Horse Association Hall of Fame. His motto as a horseman, passed to his son: "I'd rather stay friends with my horse than to go get a big check and have that horse hate me."

Adan Banuelos was born into that legacy on April 8, 1989, in Texas. He grew up on a horse ranch — a large, mixed-culture Mexican-American family where the values of family, hard work, and horsemanship were not just spoken but demonstrated every day. His late Uncle Carlos Banuelos was also a significant influence; as were Wes Adams of Western States Ranches, Kent Shiozawa, and Tim Drummond of the Drummond Ranch. His mother Tiffani was a constant presence and support.

Ascencion Banuelos
Father · First Mexican-American in NCHA Hall of Fame
Emigrated alone from Mexico to California at 13. Began by milking 400 cows twice daily on a dairy farm. Found his calling in cutting horses. Became a highly decorated trainer and the first Mexican-American ever inducted into the NCHA Hall of Fame. His philosophy: "I'd rather stay friends with my horse than go get a big check and have that horse hate me."
Adan Banuelos
Son · NCHA Hall of Fame 2017, age 28
One of the youngest NCHA Hall of Fame inductees ever. $7.4M+ NCHA lifetime earnings. Four-time Leading Open Rider. 2020 NCHA Futurity Champion. Two-time American Performance Horseman Champion. The sport's most crossover-recognized modern figure.
