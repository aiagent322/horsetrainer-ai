The Banuelos Legacy

A Dynasty Built Across Two Generations — From Mexico to the NCHA Hall of Fame

To understand Adan Banuelos, you have to start with his father. Ascencion Banuelos left Mexico alone at thirteen years old, immigrating to California in search of a better life with nothing but the determination to find it. He started on a dairy farm milking four hundred cows twice a day. From those early mornings and long days came a discipline and a work ethic that would eventually carry him to the highest honor in the cutting horse world: Ascencion Banuelos became the first Mexican-American ever inducted into the National Cutting Horse Association Hall of Fame. His motto as a horseman, passed to his son: "I'd rather stay friends with my horse than to go get a big check and have that horse hate me."

Adan Banuelos was born into that legacy on April 8, 1989, in Texas. He grew up on a horse ranch — a large, mixed-culture Mexican-American family where the values of family, hard work, and horsemanship were not just spoken but demonstrated every day. His late Uncle Carlos Banuelos was also a significant influence; as were Wes Adams of Western States Ranches, Kent Shiozawa, and Tim Drummond of the Drummond Ranch. His mother Tiffani was a constant presence and support.

The school story is not a curiosity — it is a declaration. Adan Banuelos did not learn horsemanship in a classroom. He learned it in the way his father did: by doing it, from before dawn, every day. His education was his father's barn, his father's horses, and his father's way of seeing an animal and knowing what it needs.

What Is Cutting

"Jesus, Take the Wheel" — The Sport That Demands Everything Then Lets Go

Cutting is one of Western equestrian sport's most demanding disciplines, and among its most misunderstood by those outside the horse world. A horse and rider enter a herd of cattle and select a single cow. Once the cow is separated and committed, the rider places their rein hand down on the horse's neck — and cannot lift it again. From that moment, the horse must work entirely on its own: reading the cow's moves, anticipating its turns, mirroring its attempts to escape back to the herd. Two and a half minutes of pure athleticism, instinct, and the years of training that made it possible.

Adan Banuelos describes it better than anyone: "I think something that's overlooked a lot is that we are the only equine sport that pretty much says, 'Jesus, take the wheel,' and put your hand down." The judge scores the horse — its athleticism, its control, its degree of difficulty — not the rider's technique. The rider sets the cow up, commits the horse, and then trusts. In a sport of trust, Banuelos has built a career as the rider elite horses consistently produce their best work for.

Career Record

$7.4 Million, Four Open Rider Titles, and Rewriting What's Possible at 28

Adan Banuelos turned professional in 2007 at eighteen years old. By twenty-eight he was in the NCHA Riders Hall of Fame. In 2020 he won the NCHA Futurity Open Championship — the sport's most prestigious title — on All Spice, who was named the 2021 NCHA Open Horse of the Year. He has been ranked the No. 1 NCHA Leading Open Rider four times: 2018, 2020, 2021, and 2022. His lifetime earnings have exceeded $7.4 million, making him one of the highest earners in cutting horse history.

The American Performance Horseman wins are among his most significant. The inaugural 2023 event at Globe Life Field in Arlington, Texas brought reining, cutting, and working cow horse into a single head-to-head competition. Banuelos won the cutting title — the first person ever to do so at that level — and his team (with Sarah Dawson and Fernando Salgado) won the overall competition. In 2024 he repeated: score of 230 on Storyteler, team wins again. Two inaugural years, two cutting titles, two team titles.

Culture & Crossover

The Horse World's Widest Window — From the Arena to Vogue

No cutting horse trainer — arguably no performance horse trainer of any discipline — has crossed into mainstream culture the way Adan Banuelos has. His relationship with supermodel Bella Hadid beginning in 2023 brought him into Vogue, Vanity Fair, Harper's Bazaar, and Entertainment Tonight, and with it brought the sport of cutting to audiences who had never seen a cow separated from a herd. He demonstrated bridleless cutting in the middle of New York City during Fashion Week, with Hadid beside him, as crowds stopped to watch. The documentary series "Window to the West," produced by Teton Ridge, opened with an episode about his life and career.

What distinguishes Banuelos in this crossover moment is that the sport itself comes with him. He doesn't simplify or soften cutting for outside audiences — he explains it in his own words, demonstrates it with his actual horses, and lets the drama of a working cow horse do the work. Hadid herself received NCHA Rookie of the Year honors in 2024, recognizing her as the highest-earning rookie in the association for that calendar year. The sport gained one of the most high-profile new voices it has ever had.

On social media, Banuelos has built an audience of more than 150,000 Instagram followers under @ab_performancehorses — large by any performance horse standard, and one built primarily through cutting content, training videos, and competition footage rather than lifestyle posts. The influence runs both directions: new fans discovering cutting through Banuelos, and the cutting community gaining a public face that the outside world is genuinely curious about.
