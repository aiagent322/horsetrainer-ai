Arizona Roots

Cattle Ranch Kid, Quarter Horse Jockey, University of Arizona — Then the World

Bob Baffert was born January 13, 1953, in Nogales, Arizona — a border town on the edge of the Sonoran Desert, where his family raised cattle and chickens. The middle of seven children, he grew up on a ranch and around horses from childhood. As a teenager he raced as a Quarter Horse jockey — riding in the smaller, faster-paced world of Arizona Quarter Horse racing before transitioning to training. He studied animal sciences and the Race Track Industry Program at the University of Arizona, combining formal education with the practical knowledge he had been absorbing since childhood.

His training career began on the Quarter Horse circuit in Arizona and California. He built it into something remarkable: four champion Quarter Horses, a national reputation, and a network of clients who trusted him with serious horses. By 1988 he was ready to test himself against Thoroughbreds. He made his first Thoroughbred auction purchase — a horse named Thirty Slews — and by 1992 that horse had won the Breeders' Cup Sprint. Baffert gave up his Quarter Horse division entirely in 1991, moved his operation to Santa Anita Park in southern California, and within a few years was one of the most prominent Thoroughbred trainers in the country.

He doesn't know how much he cost. But what a little racehorse.

— Bob Baffert on Medina Spirit after the 2021 Kentucky Derby win

The white hair — striking and unmistakable — and the easy confidence became his signature at the track. Baffert is married to Jill Baffert; together they have several children, including son Bode. His personality at the track is approachable and candid in a sport that often produces guarded public figures. When American Pharoah crossed the wire at Belmont in 2015, Baffert wept. The sport had not seen a Triple Crown winner since Affirmed in 1978. It had waited thirty-seven years. And it had come to Bob Baffert.

The scope of what Baffert did across these two campaigns is almost impossible to overstate. The Triple Crown — Kentucky Derby, Preakness Stakes, Belmont Stakes — had been denied to some of the greatest racehorses in history for decades. Secretariat won it in 1973. Seattle Slew in 1977. Affirmed in 1978. Then nothing for thirty-seven years. Eleven horses won two legs. None won three. Baffert's horses approached it several times: Silver Charm (1997) and Real Quiet (1998) each won the first two legs and lost the Belmont. War Emblem (2002) stumbled at the start in the Belmont. Then American Pharoah completed the sequence — and three years later, Justify did it again. In the history of the race, only Baffert and Sunny Jim Fitzsimmons have ever trained two Triple Crown winners.

Baffert also holds the all-time Preakness Stakes record with eight wins (Silver Charm 1997, Real Quiet 1998, Point Given 2001, War Emblem 2002, Lookin At Lucky 2010, I'll Have Another 2012, American Pharoah 2015, Justify 2018, National Treasure 2023). His 17 total Triple Crown race victories are the most in history.

The Medina Spirit Controversy

2021 — The Win That Was Taken Away

Medina Spirit won the 2021 Kentucky Derby by a half-length at odds of 12-1 — a horse bought as a yearling for $1,000 and purchased for Amr Zedan of Saudi Arabia for $35,000. Baffert's seventh Derby win, a new record. It was a genuine surprise — his two best horses, Life Is Good and Concert Tour, had been derailed on the road to the race. "I cannot believe he won this race," Baffert said. "That little horse, that was him, all guts. His heart is bigger than his body."

Official Record — 2021 Kentucky Derby Controversy
Shortly after the 2021 Kentucky Derby, Medina Spirit tested positive for betamethasone — a corticosteroid that is a banned substance under Kentucky racing rules. Baffert initially disputed the positive, raising alternative explanations. On February 21, 2022, the Kentucky Horse Racing Commission disqualified Medina Spirit from first place in the Kentucky Derby and elevated Mandaloun as the winner. Baffert was fined and suspended 90 days. Churchill Downs separately banned Baffert from the Kentucky Derby for three years beginning in 2022 — citing a pattern of medication violations across his horses over several years. The seven-Derby record was voided; the official count remained at six.

Medina Spirit died suddenly in December 2021 following a training exercise at Santa Anita, before the disqualification was finalized. The 2021 controversy was not Baffert's first positive test result — his horses had generated several others in the years preceding, which played into Churchill Downs's decision to impose the ban. Baffert maintained that he had been scapegoated and that the positive was the result of a topical ointment used on a skin condition. The legal and regulatory disputes played out over months. The ban held. He returned to the Derby in 2025 with Citizen Bull, which finished 15th.

Achievements

The Statistical Record of American Racing's Most Decorated Modern Trainer

  • Two Triple Crown winners: American Pharoah (2015) and Justify (2018) — only the second trainer in history to accomplish this twice; joins James "Sunny Jim" Fitzsimmons
  • Six Kentucky Derby wins: Silver Charm (1997), Real Quiet (1998), War Emblem (2002), American Pharoah (2015), Justify (2018), Authentic (2020) — tied for most with Ben A. Jones
  • Eight Preakness Stakes wins — all-time record: Silver Charm (1997), Real Quiet (1998), Point Given (2001), War Emblem (2002), Lookin At Lucky (2010), I'll Have Another (2012), American Pharoah (2015), Justify (2018), National Treasure (2023)
  • Three Belmont Stakes wins
  • 17 total Triple Crown race victories — the most in the history of American thoroughbred racing
  • American Pharoah (2015) — first Triple Crown in 37 years; also won Haskell Invitational and Breeders' Cup Classic — "Racing's Grand Slam" (first horse to accomplish all four in one year)
  • Justify (2018) — undefeated in 6 lifetime starts; Belmont win was Baffert's 15th Triple Crown race win at the time
  • Four-time Eclipse Award for Outstanding Trainer
  • National Museum of Racing and Hall of Fame inductee
  • First trainer to win the Kentucky Derby and Preakness in back-to-back years (Silver Charm 1997, Real Quiet 1998)
  • 1992 Breeders' Cup Sprint — Thirty Slews (his first major thoroughbred stakes win)
  • Three Kentucky Oaks wins; multiple Breeders' Cup wins (18+ total BC wins)
  • Born January 13, 1953, Nogales, Arizona — middle of seven children on a cattle/chicken ranch
  • University of Arizona (Race Track Industry Program / Animal Sciences)
  • Began as Quarter Horse jockey in his teens; trained four champion Quarter Horses before switching disciplines
  • Transition to thoroughbreds: first auction purchase Thirty Slews (1988); gave up Quarter Horse division in 1991
  • Wife: Jill Baffert; son Bode Baffert (and others); based at Santa Anita Park, California
  • 2021: Medina Spirit won Kentucky Derby, then disqualified after positive betamethasone test; Churchill Downs banned Baffert from Derby for three years (2022–2024)
  • Returned to Kentucky Derby in 2025 (Citizen Bull, 15th)
