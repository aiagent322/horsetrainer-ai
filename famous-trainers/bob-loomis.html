Early Life

Nebraska Beginnings & an Unlikely Path to Reining

Bob Loomis grew up in Nebraska in a family whose equestrian roots ran in a decidedly different direction than the Western stock-horse world he would come to define. His family was involved with hunters, jumpers, and gaited horses — the refined English disciplines, not the sliding stops and spins of the reining pen. "I knew that someday I wanted to train and show hunters and jumpers," Bob has said. But horses were horses, and the passion was already there, waiting to find its discipline.

That passion led him, in ways both deliberate and accidental, into the world of Quarter Horses and Western performance. He became a student of horses broadly — studying technique, conformation, movement, and the subtle communication between rider and horse — before he ever committed to a single path. That broad foundation would later set him apart: a reining trainer who thought in terms of the whole horse, not just the maneuver.

When I was 23 years old in 1966, that's when I started training professionally, and I've loved every minute of it since.

— Bob Loomis

In the early 1970s, his reputation was already building in Nebraska and beyond. Fellow horsemen from the West Coast made pilgrimages to watch him work — studying the accuracy of his circles, the precision of his patterns, the balance he found between softness and collection. He became a reference point for an entire generation of aspiring reiners before most of them had ever met him.

Professional Career

Six Futurities and a Record That Stands Alone

Bob Loomis began training reining horses professionally in 1966, at age 23. His first notable public recognition came in 1972 when he was co-reserve champion at the NRHA Futurity on Britton Princess. It was a signal of what was coming. Over the next two decades, he would claim the most prestigious championship in the sport six times — a record that has never been matched.

His six NRHA Open Futurity Championship titles, won over a span of a decade, were each achieved on a different horse — a testament not to one exceptional animal, but to a training system so sound and repeatable that it could produce champions reliably, year after year. The record speaks to both the skill of the horseman and the discipline of the program he built.

Beyond the Futurity, Loomis won 12 world championships across NRHA and AQHA competition, the NRHA Derby, the NRHA Superstakes, and trained and showed numerous other AQHA and NRHA world champions ridden by other competitors. He was elected president of the National Reining Horse Association in 1982 — a recognition by his peers of his leadership role in shaping the sport itself, not merely competing in it.

Breeding Legacy

Three Generations of Million Dollar Sires

Bob Loomis began his breeding program in 1974, driven by a simple and powerful motivation: nobody was breeding the kind of horses he wanted to ride. He started by crossing quality mares — particularly his Boss' Nowata Star line — and in 1978 made the purchase that would define his bloodline legacy. He bought a three-year-old stallion named Topsail Cody.

What followed over the next four decades is one of the most remarkable breeding achievements in the history of Western performance horses. Loomis is the only breeder in history to have trained, shown, and stood three consecutive generations of NRHA Million Dollar Sires — a dynasty of stud horses whose influence runs through virtually every competitive reining horse in the world today.

Topsail Cody, bred by CT Fuller of Catasauqua, Pennsylvania, was a stallion built on classic Quarter Horse bloodlines — Wimpy, King, and Doc Bar on his papers. Loomis trained and showed him to win the NRHA Futurity in 1980 and the AQHA Junior Reining World Championship in 1981. He was inducted into the NRHA Hall of Fame in 1996 and became only the fourth stallion to reach NRHA One Million Dollar Sire status in 1998.

Topsail Whiz was foaled in April 1987 on the Loomis Ranch at Marietta, Oklahoma, by Topsail Cody and out of Jeanie Whiz Bar. "Topsail Whiz was just a gorgeous colt from the day he was born," Bob has said. "He was a beautiful mover, he loped so pretty, and you couldn't help but notice him in the pasture when he was growing up." Whiz competed under Loomis at six NRHA events from 1990 to 1993, earning nearly $50,000, including a third-place finish at the 1990 NRHA Futurity. But his true impact came in the breeding shed. He became the NRHA's all-time leading sire and, in 2018, the first stallion in the association's history to reach $12 million in offspring earnings. He sired seven NRHA Open Futurity champions, four NRHA Open Derby champions, and three NRHA Million Dollar Sires — his children becoming as dominant as he was. Topsail Whiz passed away in 2009.

There are two kinds of people who ride horses. There are people who really love a horse, and then there are the people who love what a horse can do for them. You fit in one category or the other.

— Bob Loomis
Philosophy & Book

Reining: The Art of Performance in Horses

In 1990, Bob Loomis co-authored what would become the definitive textbook of the reining discipline: Reining: The Art of Performance in Horses, written with Kathy Kadash and illustrated by John Hillenbrand, published by Equimedia. At 222 pages, the book laid out Loomis's complete training philosophy and methodology in the most systematic treatment the sport had yet received.

The book's central philosophy can be distilled into a single phrase that Loomis made his own: think, don't intimidate. Rather than training through pressure, submission, or force, Loomis argued for building a horse's understanding step by step — earning the maneuver rather than demanding it. He covers the full arc of development: the importance of breeding and conformation, proper ground work, basic training foundations, conditioning (with his famous axiom "never let your horse run out of air"), suppling exercises, body alignment through circles, and the advanced maneuvers that define the reining pattern.

The book is notable for being genuinely accessible — as useful to the serious amateur rider as to the professional trainer. Reviewers noted that Loomis succeeded where many training books fail: he found words for the feel of the horse, the subtle physical communication that can't be diagrammed but must be developed through thousands of hours in the saddle. The breadth of the book — it also covers shoeing, equipment selection, and show preparation — reflected a horseman who understood that excellence in the arena begins long before the horse ever enters the gate.

For a generation of reining trainers, Reining: The Art of Performance in Horses became the foundational text — required reading, dog-eared and marked up, kept in tack rooms and trailer compartments across North America.

Achievements

A Career Without Parallel in the Reining World

  • Six-time NRHA Open Futurity Champion (1976, 1978, 1979, 1980, 1984, 1986) — the all-time record, never surpassed
  • 12 World Championships across NRHA and AQHA competition
  • NRHA Hall of Fame inductee — 1992
  • AQHA Hall of Fame inductee — 2012
  • President of the National Reining Horse Association — 1982
  • Only breeder in history to train, show, and stand three consecutive generations of NRHA Million Dollar Sires: Topsail Cody, Topsail Whiz, and West Coast Whiz

  • Author of Reining: The Art of Performance in Horses (1990) — the defining text of the discipline
  • Trained hundreds of reining champions across five decades of professional training
