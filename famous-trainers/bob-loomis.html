Early Life

Nebraska-Born, Hunter-Jumper Roots, and an Unusual Path to Reining

Bob Loomis was born in 1943 in Nebraska — but he did not grow up in the world of western performance horses. His family background was in hunters, jumpers, and gaited horses, the English-influenced disciplines of the Midwest show world. "I knew that someday I wanted to train and show hunters and jumpers," he has said of his early ambitions. It was a start that gave him a technical foundation in precision, balance, and the communication between horse and rider that would serve him for the rest of his career — even as he redirected that foundation into an entirely different discipline.

His pivot to reining came through study and observation. In the early 1970s he rode with great stock-horse trainers on the West Coast and simultaneously studied respected reiners in other parts of the country, visiting Lincoln, Nebraska, to watch competition at the AQHA World Championship Show and the All-American Quarter Horse Congress. From these influences he absorbed the importance of accurate, round circles — the hallmark of the Loomis competitive style — and the discipline of precision that would become his signature in the arena.

There are two kinds of people who ride horses. There are people who really love a horse, and then there are the people who love what a horse can do for them. You fit in one category or the other.

— Bob Loomis

He began training reining horses professionally in 1966 — the inaugural year of the NRHA — and earned his first public notice as a reining trainer in 1972 when he was co-reserve champion at the NRHA Futurity on Britton Princess. From there, the wins came in rapid succession. He also began his breeding program in 1974, laying the foundation for what would become the most influential sire dynasty in reining history.

The Futurity Record

Six NRHA Open Futurity Championships — More Than Any Rider in History

Bob Loomis has won the NRHA Open Futurity six times — more than any other rider in the history of the sport. That record has never been matched. The six championships span a fifteen-year period from 1976 through 1990, with three consecutive wins from 1978 to 1980 that stand as the most dominant three-year stretch in Futurity history.

Lady El Dorado — the 1978 champion — holds a unique distinction: she is the only horse in NRHA history to win the Futurity as a three-year-old and then return the following year to win the NRHA Derby (then called the Maturity). That back-to-back achievement on the same horse across two consecutive years of major competition is without precedent in the sport. Miss Della Doc is the only horse Loomis won the Futurity on twice — in 1984 and 1990, six years apart — both at the $100,000 level. In 1976, the same year as his first Futurity championship, Loomis also won the All-American Quarter Horse Congress Open Reining on High Proof — a complete double-header reining year.

The Breeding Dynasty

Three Generations of Million Dollar Sires — A Record Without Equal

Bob Loomis began his breeding program in 1974, using his Boss' Nowata Star mares as the foundation. Four years later he acquired Topsail Cody as a three-year-old — originally for his brother Ed — and recognized the horse's potential before anyone else did. When Ed took the colt briefly and Loomis got him back in the spring of his futurity year, he bought Topsail Cody back at four times the original price. He showed the horse just twice before the 1980 NRHA Futurity, winning both times, then won the Futurity itself. Topsail Cody became an NRHA World Champion in junior reining in 1981, an NRHA Hall of Fame inductee in 1996, and eventually an NRHA Million Dollar Sire.

What Loomis built over the next three decades is without parallel in the sport's history: three consecutive generations of NRHA Million Dollar Sires, all bred, trained, shown, and stood at his own operation — the only such dynasty in reining history.

Topsail Whiz's offspring earnings exceeded $12 million — making him reining's all-time leading sire, the first $12 Million Sire in NRHA history. He sired seven NRHA Open Futurity champions, four NRHA Open Derby champions, three more Million Dollar Sires, and two Two Million Dollar Sires. Bob Loomis remains the only breeder to have trained, shown, and stood three consecutive generations of NRHA Million Dollar Sires.

Philosophy & Legacy

Old School, Horse First — The Loomis Standard

Bob Loomis's philosophy is direct: the horse's health and well-being is first. Not the pattern, not the score, not the paycheck. He is self-described as "old school" — not as nostalgia but as principle. He wants to see horses lope circles with their necks flat, not artificially elevated or forced into a frame that doesn't suit their structure. "I'm all about the horse," he has said. "For me when I'm training a horse, his health and well-being is No. 1."

He wrote Reining: The Art of Performance in Horses — one of the foundational texts of modern reining instruction, covering horse psychology, training technique, and the mechanics of precision performance. He served as NRHA President in 1982, guiding the association through a period of rapid growth as the sport expanded beyond its Ohio origins. He was inducted into the NRHA Hall of Fame in 1992 and the AQHA Hall of Fame thereafter.

No matter the discipline, my desire is always to excel. I'm old school. I'm all about the horse. I don't want to see it down on that damn ground. I like to see horses lope circles with their neck flat.

— Bob Loomis

He lives at his ranch in Overbook, Oklahoma, where he continues to raise horses and Texas Longhorn cattle. His influence on the competitive and breeding landscape of modern reining is total — in the arena through six Futurity championships that no one has matched, and in the breeding shed through a three-generation sire dynasty that continues producing champions long after the horses themselves are gone.
