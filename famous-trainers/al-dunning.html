Early Life

Chicago to the Sonoran Desert — A Boy and the Arizona Horse World

Al Dunning was born on March 5, 1950, in Chicago, Illinois — about as far from the Arizona desert and the Western performance horse world as a person can start. When he was eight years old, his family moved to Arizona, and the trajectory of his life changed permanently. He began riding horses with his sisters and quickly discovered a natural feel and competitive drive that would define fifty-plus years of professional horsemanship.

By age twelve he was already earning his way in the horse world — picking oranges for a restaurant in Scottsdale, doing whatever work was available. He began formal riding and horsemanship instruction at Siminoff Stables, where he learned the foundations of Western performance, roping, and horsemanship. From 1962 to 1968 he showed at both AQHA and American Horse Show Association shows in reining, working cow horse, horsemanship, and other events — building the competitive foundation he would stand on for the next five decades.

Leadership & Industry

Shaping Arizona's Horse World From the Inside

Al Dunning's contribution to the horse industry goes far beyond what he's accomplished in the show pen. He has been a builder of the institutions that govern and grow the sport he loves. He is one of the founding members of the Arizona Quarter Horse Youth Association and served as its Charter President. He has served as president of both the Arizona Quarter Horse Association and the Arizona Cutting Horse Association. He is a Director at Large with the AQHA, past Chairman of the Judges Committee, and a member of the Hall of Fame Committee. With the NCHA he has served as a National Director and on the Nominations, Limited Age Events, and Rules Committees. He is an NRCHA-carded judge who has judged the Snaffle Bit Futurity four times and the Derby three times.

His wife Becky co-founded America's Horse Cares, a division of the AQHA Foundation focused on therapeutic riding charities — extending the Dunning family's commitment to horses beyond the competitive arena and into the community.

He has mentored a generation of professional trainers who have gone on to their own successful careers: Brad Barkemeyer, Patrick Flaherty, Chris Johnsrud, Casey Hinton, Pete Kyle, John Pipkin, Brett Stone, Mike Wood, Jade Keller, and Cody Lamont are among those who came through Almosta Ranch. "I have so many people to thank for helping me to be a success," Dunning has said — an acknowledgment that works both ways.

Books, Media & Education

Author, Clinician, and Online Educator

Al Dunning has committed his knowledge to print in five books that span the full range of his expertise. His landmark book Reining: The Guide for Training & Showing Winning Reining Horses — published by Western Horseman, edited by the late Pat Close, and photographed by the late Kathy Swan — has sold over 100,000 copies worldwide and is recognized as one of the finest books ever published on horse training. Its extraordinary range of images by Swan captures every stage of the training process with a clarity and artistry that has made the book as much a visual record as a technical manual. Expensive Hobby, the great buckskin, appears on the cover of the second edition. Dunning's other titles cover the hackamore, working cow horse, and the broader philosophy of horsemanship at its highest level.

He has produced six instructional videos covering cutting, reining, a riders' guide, advancing to the hackamore, and fundamental training of the young horse. He has authored countless articles for Western Horseman and Horse & Rider, and has judged the AQHA World Show three times. His long-running presence at clinics worldwide — from Australia to Europe to across North America — has made him one of the most widely traveled instructors in the Western discipline world.

In recent years Dunning has expanded into online education with Team AD International, a digital mentoring and lesson program, and AD Tack, an online store for tack and training equipment. He hosts the Almosta Cowboy podcast, where he sits down with trainers, competitors, and industry figures for in-depth conversations about horsemanship, competition, and the business of horses. The show reflects what has always been true about Al Dunning: he is as interested in the next generation as in his own legacy.
