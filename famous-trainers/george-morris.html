Background

New York Foxhunting Family to America's Foremost Equitation Champion

George H. Morris was born on February 26, 1938, in New York and grew up in a family involved with horses and foxhunting. He began riding at a young age and quickly became active in the American hunter and equitation system. Equitation is a discipline focused on rider position, effectiveness, and horsemanship rather than solely the horse's performance.

During the 1950s, Morris became one of the most successful junior equitation riders in the United States. He won several of the nation's top equitation championships, including the ASPCA Maclay Finals, the AHSA Medal Finals, and the USET Talent Search Finals. His achievements established him as one of the leading young riders of his generation and contributed to his early reputation as a technically skilled horseman.

Olympic Competition

1960 Rome Olympics — Representing the United States in Show Jumping

Morris later transitioned into international show jumping competition. In 1960, he represented the United States at the Olympic Games in Rome as part of the U.S. show jumping team. He also competed in major international events including Nations Cup competitions and other elite jumping events during the 1960s. His international riding career occurred during a period when American equestrian sport was becoming increasingly competitive on the world stage.

Although Morris had success as a competitor, he became even more influential through his work as a trainer, instructor, and coach. Throughout the 1970s and 1980s, he trained numerous top hunter, jumper, and equitation riders in the United States. His teaching style emphasized classical riding principles, rider discipline, balance, effectiveness, and correct fundamentals derived from European riding traditions.

Coaching & Education

Chef d'Équipe, Hunter Seat Equitation, and a Generation of International Riders

Morris became closely associated with the American Hunter Seat Equitation system and played a major role in shaping modern equitation standards. Many riders who trained under him later became successful international competitors, trainers, and instructors themselves. Through clinics and training sessions, his influence extended throughout the broader hunter/jumper industry in North America.

In 1988, Morris became Chef d'Équipe of the United States Show Jumping Team. During his tenure, the U.S. team won multiple medals at Olympic Games, World Equestrian Games, and Pan American Games competitions. He remained a central figure in U.S. international jumping through the 1990s and early 2000s.

Morris also became widely known through his clinics, instructional publications, and educational videos. His book Hunter Seat Equitation became one of the most recognized instructional texts in American riding education and has been used extensively by riders, trainers, and equestrian programs. He also wrote columns and instructional articles for major equestrian publications including Practical Horseman.

Controversy

SafeSport Sanctions — 2019

In later years, Morris became the subject of controversy involving allegations of sexual misconduct and SafeSport sanctions within the equestrian industry. The United States Center for SafeSport permanently banned Morris from participation in equestrian sport under its jurisdiction in 2019. Those developments became part of the broader public discussion surrounding athlete safety and governance in equestrian sports.
