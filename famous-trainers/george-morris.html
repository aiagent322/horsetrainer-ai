Background

George Morris

George Hayes Morris was born on February 26, 1938. He grew up in New Canaan, Connecticut. His parents were Alice and Harry Morris. He attended the University of Virginia. He has been described as having suffered from a fear of horses during childhood. He began competing in the ASPCA Maclay Finals at Madison Square Garden in 1950, though he did not place that year. He began riding with legendary teacher Gordon Wright and got his first great horse, Game Cock. In 1952 at age 14 he won both the ASPCA Maclay Horsemanship Finals and the AHSA Hunt Seat Equitation Medal Finals — the youngest rider ever to do so. He is considered a founding father of hunt seat equitation.

In 1957 he joined the USET on a national summer tour. Between 1958 and 1960 he rode on eight winning Nations Cup teams. In 1959 he was a member of the gold medal-winning US team at the Pan American Games in Chicago, alongside Frank Chapot, Hugh Wiley, and Bill Steinkraus. At the 1960 Rome Olympics he was the youngest member of the US show jumping team, which won the team silver medal; he placed fourth individually. After the 1960 Olympics he retired from competition and briefly pursued an acting career, returning to showing in 1963 as a professional. In 1971 he bought property in Pittstown, New Jersey that became Hunterdon stables. That same year he authored Hunter Seat Equitation, a text widely regarded as one of the most influential in the history of the discipline.

Coaching Career

Chef d'Equipe and Training

Morris trained students including Conrad Homfeld, who won the Medal and Maclay finals in 1967. At the 1984 Los Angeles Olympics, three of his former students — Conrad Homfeld, Leslie Burr-Howard, and Melanie Smith Taylor — were members of the gold medal-winning US team. His students Lisa Jacquin, Anne Kursinski, and Norman Dello Joio competed at the 1992 Barcelona Olympics. In 1996 he served as assistant Chef d'Equipe of the silver medal Olympic team, which included former students Burr-Howard, Kursinski, and Peter Leone. Former student Chris Kappler won the individual silver medal and was a member of the gold medal team at the 2004 Athens Olympics.

Morris fully assumed the position of Chef d'Equipe of the United States show jumping team in 2005 and held the role until 2013, when he was replaced by Robert Ridland. Under his leadership the US show jumping team won Olympic gold in 2008. He served as coach for the Brazilian show jumping team at the 2016 Rio Olympics. He has served as president of the United States Show Jumping Hall of Fame and on the USEF National Jumper Committee and Planning Committee.
