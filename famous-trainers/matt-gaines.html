The Gaines Legacy

Son of the Only Man in All Three NCHA Halls of Fame

Matt Gaines grew up in Weatherford, Texas in one of cutting's most storied families. His father, Dick Gaines, is the only person in the entire history of the National Cutting Horse Association to be inducted into all three of its Halls of Fame — a distinction so singular that it may never be replicated. A past NCHA President, Dick Gaines was inducted into the Non-Pro Hall of Fame in 1980, the Rider's Hall of Fame in 1992, and the Member's Hall of Fame in 2005 for his lifelong service and contributions to the sport. He passed away in January 2019.

The Legacy — Dick Gaines, 1943–2019
Dick Gaines
The Only Person Inducted Into All Three NCHA Halls of Fame
Past President of the National Cutting Horse Association. Known not just for his expertise but for his dedication, humor, and the lasting friendships he built across the sport.
  • NCHA Non-Pro Hall of Fame — 1980
  • NCHA Rider's Hall of Fame — 1992
  • NCHA Member's Hall of Fame — 2005 (for service; nominated by peers)

Despite growing up with a Hall of Fame father who trained cutting horses, Matt's parents urged him to go to college rather than pursue professional training. He earned an agriculture business degree from Tarleton State University, then went to work for tack manufacturer Dennis Moreland. But his heart was never in business. He eventually moved to Mississippi to take a job as a cutting horse trainer under the late Charles Spence, and never looked back. Before turning professional, however, he had already established himself as a standout non-pro — winning the NCHA Non-Pro Futurity in 1990 on Playboys Lynnea.

He and his wife Tara, an NCHA Non-Pro World Champion, operate Matt Gaines Cutting Horses together in Weatherford, Texas.

The Score — 234

World Record Tied — Special Nu Baby and the Best Run He Ever Had

September 12, 2015 · El Rancho Futurity · Rancho Murieta, California
234
All-Time NCHA World Record (tied) · Special Nu Baby & Matt Gaines
"That mare is just absolutely amazing. She's just one of a kind. That's the best run I've ever had."

On September 12, 2015, at the Mercuria/NCHA World Series of Cutting Open finals at the El Rancho Futurity in Rancho Murieta, California, Matt Gaines and Special Nu Baby came from the sixth draw in an eight-horse finals to deliver a run that the crowd and the judges will not forget. The score: 234 — tying the all-time NCHA world record set by Dont Look Twice and Phil Rapp in 2011. It was the defining moment of a year — and a partnership — already full of them.

Special Nu Baby was a mare Gaines had ridden for her entire career, from the beginning. By Dual Rey and out of Nu I Wood — a mare Gaines had previously won the NCHA Derby with in 2001 — she was owned by Gary and Shannon Barker of Madill, Oklahoma, and bred by Crystal Creek Ranch. Over the course of 2015, she and Gaines won five Mercuria/NCHA World Series of Cutting Championships, including four consecutive events. She won the 2015 NCHA Open World Championship in Fort Worth. And on that September evening in California, she scored 234 to tie the sport's highest-ever mark. "She's just one of a kind," Gaines said. "That's the best run I've ever had."

Special Nu Baby earned $481,197 in her show career and was inducted into the NCHA Hall of Fame. Her dam Nu I Wood — the horse Gaines had won the 2001 NCHA Derby on — became one of the sport's great producers, with more than $950,000 in progeny earnings.

One of Matt Gaines' most distinctive career achievements is being one of only three riders in NCHA history to have shown two NCHA Open Horse of the Year winners: Little Pepto Gal (2002) and Smooth As A Cat (2005). The Horse of the Year award represents the highest-earning horse in NCHA Open competition for the calendar year — winning it once is rare. Winning it twice, on different horses, is a testament to the consistent depth of talent in Gaines' program.

His training philosophy is built on consistency: "Consistency is the key when it comes to your position on a cow and trying to teach the horse the same thing every day until they get it." And on heart in horses: "You can't make them more athletic. They either have heart or they don't. All the special horses have a lot of heart." His favorite quote: "You can't win if you're afraid to lose."
