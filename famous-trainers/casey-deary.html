Early Life — The Self-Made Story

Born 1980 in Texas, No Horses in the Family — Built It All from Nothing

Casey Deary was born in 1980 in Texas to parents who did not own horses. No ranch background, no family tradition in the show pen, no inherited equipment or connections. What he had were parents who taught him the values of God, family, hard work, integrity, and helping others — and a drive to pursue a life around horses that nobody around him had built before him.

He found horses as a young man and pursued the cowboy life methodically and on his own terms: buying his own truck, his own trailer, his own saddle, and his own first horse one piece at a time. By age sixteen he had already identified a business model — purchasing low-cost unbroken horses, training them, and reselling them. The profit margin was thin and the work was hard, but it was his. He was building a training business before he had a driver's license for long.

I feel like God put me here to ride these horses and use these opportunities to spread His word. Despite the wins and the millions, the work has always meant something beyond the scoreboard.

— Casey Deary

A formative turning point came when he attended a clinic put on by James Davidson, who worked under legendary NRHA Hall of Famer Dick Pieper. One thing Davidson said landed permanently: "If a guy can learn to ride a horse like a reiner, he can transition that horse into any event." Deary set his sights on that mastery, working summer vacations under Davidson at Pieper's ranch in Ardmore, Oklahoma. Then, the day after high school graduation, he took up full-time residence with famed trainer Clint Haverty. He also attended junior college night classes in business — combining the demanding days at work with long evenings of study. He was building every component of his future simultaneously.

After approximately three years with Haverty — learning how to run a reining business, how to care for and develop a show horse, and how to compete at the professional level — he burned out. After the 2001 NRHA Derby, he asked for two weeks off, drove to Nicole's parents' cabin in Glenrose, Texas, and spent the time doing nothing. It was exactly what he needed. He told the Havertys he wasn't coming back. He was ready to go out on his own.

Building Deary Performance Horses

$36.28, Katy, Texas — and a Long Road to the Top

In 2000, Casey Deary earned his first National Reining Horse Association check: $36.28, on a horse named Crome Marks, at a reining in Katy, Texas. It was about enough for a nice steak dinner with Nicole. But it was a check. It was money earned doing what he had set out to do.

Casey and Nicole had met while showing horses in high school — he showed Quarter Horses, she showed Paints. They parted ways as young people do, and Nicole went through what she described as her "rebellious period" in college. They ran into each other again at the American Paint Horse Association World Show. The encounter rekindled their friendship, and eventually their relationship. They married and are raising four children — Wyatt, Owen, Joy, and Olivia — in Weatherford, Texas, where they founded Deary Performance Horses in 2000.

The early years were lean. Deary consistently made major NRHA event finals, but the transformative win — the one that changed the financial and competitive trajectory of his career — came in 2012. "Americasnextgunmodel was kind of my first elite athlete," he has said. "Not that I didn't have good ones before that, but she got me to the level that I wanted to be at." She was a mare owned by Arcese Quarter Horses that Nicole essentially demanded they pursue at the two-year-old sale. "She all but threatened me to make sure that one didn't get away," Deary recalled. Nicole's instinct proved prescient.

The Championship Record

Four NRHA Futurities Across Thirteen Years — The Deary Dynasty

Casey Deary has won the NRHA Level 4 Open Futurity four times — in 2012, 2015, 2021, and 2025. Each title came on a different horse, across a thirteen-year span, each at a different stage of his career and earning power. Together they form one of the most consistent records of elite performance in modern reining history.

The 2025 Futurity win on Belissimo — a stallion by NRHA Hall of Famer Magnum Chic Dream out of Americasnextgunmodel, bred and owned by Arcese Quarter Horses — is among the most poetically complete wins in the sport's recent history: the son of the mare who launched Deary's elite career, carrying him to a fourth Futurity championship thirteen years later. His son Owen finished a standout non-pro performance the night before, including $104,000+ across divisions. The Deary family had the arena that week.

The Americasnextgunmodel Dynasty

One Mare, Thirteen Years, $6 Million — The Thread That Runs Through Everything

No single horse, or family of horses, has shaped Casey Deary's career the way Americasnextgunmodel and her descendants have. The story begins with Nicole's instinct at a two-year-old sale and runs through four Futurity finalists, multiple Derby champions, and a legacy that will produce champions long after Deary has retired.

The dynasty is not just competitive — it reflects a consistent partnership with Arcese Quarter Horses, the breeding program that produced Americasnextgunmodel, Americasnexttopgun, and Belissimo. "That's such a special mare," Deary said of Americasnextgunmodel. The thread connecting his 2012 breakthrough win to his 2025 fourth title runs directly through her: the mare Nicole spotted at the two-year-old sale, the mare Deary rode to the title that changed everything.

Television & Public Profile

The Last Cowboy, Run For A Million, and Reining's Most Beloved Family

Casey and Nicole Deary, along with their four children, have been featured prominently in The Last Cowboy — Taylor Sheridan's CMT/Paramount documentary-competition series that brought professional reining to mainstream audiences starting in 2019. The Deary family's combination of competitive excellence, Christian faith, genuine warmth, and the drama of high-stakes competition made them natural protagonists. Audiences who had never watched a reining pattern before found themselves invested in the Dearys' journey.

Since the Run for a Million's inception in 2019 — a $1 million reining competition at the South Point Arena in Las Vegas, Nevada, created by Taylor Sheridan — Deary has competed in the event every single year, one of a small group of elite riders to qualify consistently for the annual showcase. In 2022, he won the Run for a Million Shootout Class on Down Right Amazing, qualifying him for the main event. He has also served as a commentator for the event, bringing his expertise and personality to audiences watching the sport at its most televised moment.

I knew I was close, but I wasn't exactly sure how close. I had three horses that could all potentially win the deal. It wasn't stressful, it was exciting. That's what we all work for at a show.

— Casey Deary, on crossing $2M at the 2020 NRBC

Deary's public presence is one of authenticity. He speaks openly about his faith — "I feel like God put me here to ride these horses and use these opportunities to spread His word" — and about his family as the foundation of everything he has built. God, family, and business. Despite four Futurity championships and $6 million in career earnings, he remains, by the consistent account of those who know him, genuinely humble about his place among the sport's elite riders.
