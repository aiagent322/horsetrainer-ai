Roots & Rise

From California to Oklahoma — The Making of an Eight-Time World Champion

Joe Heim was born in 1949 and found cutting horses in California, where he built his early career before making a pivotal move to southern Oklahoma. When the Heims relocated from California to Thackerville, Oklahoma — a small community in Love County near the Texas border — it was a defining moment both for the family and for their most important horse. Joice Heim bred and raised a foal that was born one month after that move. She named him "Okie" in honor of the occasion. The full name: Docs Okie Quixote.

Joe Heim began competing seriously in the mid-1970s and qualified for the NCHA Futurity finals for the first time in 1977, at age 28. Riding Doc's Serendipity, he finished as Reserve Champion — losing by half a point to Peppy San Badger and Buster Welch. That near-miss became a motivating benchmark. "He was right there," the sport recognized. The 1980 Futurity saw him win the semi-finals on Miss Daube Ritz with a 224, before tying for fifth in the finals. Then came 1981 — and Colonel Lil.

He was a small horse and worked with a low profile, with his head and body low. And he was real intelligent and cow-smart. When we went to the Futurity, he had a lot of maturity about him.

— Joe Heim on Docs Okie Quixote

His career path from California to Thackerville ultimately produced one of the most singular achievements in NCHA history — not just the Triple Crown, but the specific nature of how it was accomplished. The NCHA Triple Crown had been won once before, by Smart Little Lena with Bill Freeman. But Heim's version was different in a way that has never been replicated: he bred Docs Okie Quixote with his wife Joice, owned the horse himself, trained the horse himself, and rode every championship run himself. One man, one family, one horse — all the way.

The Road to the Triple Crown

Seven Futurities, Two Championships, One Historic Run

Joe Heim's path to the NCHA Futurity championship was built through persistent, patient pursuit — qualifying year after year, learning what the finals required, and arriving with the right horse at the right time. His journey to his first Futurity title spanned multiple years and multiple horses before Colonel Lil and then Docs Okie Quixote delivered back-to-back championships in 1981 and 1983.

The 1981 Futurity title on Colonel Lil — a daughter of Colonel Freckles, owned by W.D. Wood — established Heim as a Futurity-caliber trainer. But the 1983 win on Docs Okie Quixote was, as Heim has said himself, the more special one. Joice had bred and raised the horse. Joe had trained him from the beginning. The Futurity win, worth $263,483, was the first leg of what would become only the second NCHA Triple Crown in history.

The NCHA Triple Crown — consisting of the NCHA Futurity, the NCHA Super Stakes, and the NCHA Derby — has been won only three times in the association's history. Smart Little Lena won it first, ridden by Bill Freeman (1982–83). Docs Okie Quixote won it second, with Joe Heim (1983–84). Chiquita Pistol won it third, ridden by Tag Rice (2002–03). What distinguishes the Heim–Docs Okie Quixote Triple Crown from the others is total in its uniqueness: Joice Heim bred the stallion, Joe and Joice owned him throughout his career, Joe trained him from the start, and Joe rode every winning run. It is the only time in NCHA history that one person (with their family) was the breeder, owner, trainer, and rider of a Triple Crown horse.

Going into the 1984 Derby, Heim had no doubt that Docs Okie Quixote was capable of the win. The stallion scored 221.5 to complete the Triple Crown three months after the Super Stakes. His Triple Crown earnings totaled $335,095. He was inducted into the NCHA Horse Hall of Fame. Then, in 1985, at age five, Docs Okie Quixote died — having sired only one crop of foals. What those progeny might have produced at scale will never be known. The sport lost one of its rarest individuals.

After his peak competition years, Joe Heim continued as both a competitor and clinician at Joe Heim Quarter Horses, Inc. in Thackerville, Oklahoma. He offers custom training courses for intermediate to advanced riders, non-pros, and professional trainers — an "8-time World Champion" with insider knowledge of what it takes to win at the highest level. His training approach spans multiple western disciplines: NCHA cutting, AQHA performance horses, and NRHA reining. His guarantee to clinic students: get you to the next level.

His wife Joice — who bred Docs Okie Quixote and whose name was attached to the horse's most celebrated story — remained central to the operation. Wikipedia lists his spouse as Holly, suggesting a later marriage. The community of Thackerville, in Love County on the Oklahoma-Texas border, remains his home base.
