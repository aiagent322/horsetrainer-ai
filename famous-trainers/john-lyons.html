Early Life

Colorado's Western Slope — A Late Discovery and a Lifelong Calling

John Lyons was born in 1947 in the United States, and came to horses by an unusual route: he discovered them as an adult. Unlike most of the great horsemen in this series — who grew up on ranches or in the saddle from childhood — Lyons found horses at around age twenty-four, while working a cattle ranch on Divide Creek near Silt on Colorado's Western Slope. By his own telling, the encounter was immediate and total. "It's a disease, worse than probably any addiction," he would later say of his love for horses. He was hooked from the start.

That late start gave him something valuable: he came to horses without the ingrained assumptions of someone who had grown up around them. He had to think through everything from first principles — why did the horse do what it did, what did the horse need to understand, how could a human communicate clearly enough that the horse could follow? He had a background in education, and that background shaped his entire approach. He thought not like a horseman who had always ridden, but like a teacher who wanted every student to succeed.

I decided to help people. Not just train their horses for them. Because what this guy was teaching was so dangerous — and I knew there had to be a better way.

— John Lyons, on attending a horse clinic in Eagle, Colorado

The catalyst for his career as a clinician came from witnessing what he didn't want to be. He attended a training clinic in Eagle, Colorado, and was disturbed by what he saw — methods built on fear, punishment, and dangerous pressure. "What this guy was teaching was so dangerous and terrible," he recalled. He walked out resolved: he would develop a safer, cleaner, more effective system, and he would teach it not by training people's horses for them, but by teaching people how to train their own horses. That distinction — teaching the person rather than just fixing the horse — would define his entire career.

Philosophy & Method

Conditioned Response — Safety, Clarity, and the Science of Learning

The Lyons Method is grounded in the principles of operant conditioning — the science of how animals learn through the association of behaviors with consequences. Rather than relying on fear, dominance, or dramatic pressure techniques, Lyons built a system around clear signals, consistent reinforcement, and measurable goals. If you know exactly what you're asking, can signal it cleanly, and reward the correct response every time, the horse will learn. The approach demystifies training, making it accessible to anyone willing to be consistent.

What distinguishes Lyons from many of his contemporaries is his emphasis on safety above all else. He deliberately avoids spectacular, dramatic demonstrations in favor of methodical, teachable progressions. He sets specific goals — not vague aspirations — and teaches them one step at a time. The horse is never over-faced. The handler is never put in danger. The result is a horse that is reliable, consistent, and willing — and a handler who understands what they're doing and why.

His method is built on five interlocking principles that work together to create a lasting, positive partnership:

Safety First
Never ask the horse or rider to do something unsafe. Build trust by keeping every interaction within the comfort zone of both.
Clear Signals
The horse can only respond correctly if the cue is consistent and comprehensible. Clean communication is the foundation of everything.
Specific Goals
Vague aspirations fail. Teach one specific behavior at a time, completely, before moving to the next. Progress is measurable.
Consistent Reinforcement
Reward the right response every time. The horse learns through pattern recognition — consistency creates conditioned responses.
Patience & Repetition
Lasting learning takes thousands of correct repetitions. There are no shortcuts — only clear, patient, consistent work.

This framework — drawn from behavioral science and honed across forty-plus years and more than 100,000 horses — gave everyday horse owners something they had never had before: a system they could understand, explain to themselves, and replicate at home. Not magic. Not an inexplicable gift. Science applied to relationship.

Career

From Divide Creek to Every Expo in the World

After gaining practical horsemanship knowledge on his Colorado cattle ranch, Lyons went on to build a successful show career before turning his focus to teaching. In 1980, he gave his first training clinic and symposium — and discovered immediately that people were hungry for what he was offering. He had a background in education, and he understood that hunger. He knew how to break down complex concepts into teachable sequences, how to read a room and adjust his delivery, how to make a struggling student feel capable rather than lost.

The clinics multiplied. The reputation grew. By the mid-1980s he was one of the most sought-after horse clinicians in America, operating alongside Pat Parelli at the forefront of the natural horsemanship movement. Unlike many clinicians who worked primarily with professionals, Lyons targeted the vast middle ground: the millions of ordinary horse owners who loved their animals but didn't know how to communicate with them, who were frightened by their horses' behavior and didn't understand why it was happening.

He became the teacher of teachers. He and his son Josh developed a certification program that produced more than 300 professional John & Josh Lyons Certified Trainers across the United States and in many other countries. That network of certified trainers — each trained in the Lyons system, each accountable to its principles of safety and clarity — extended the reach of the method far beyond what any single clinician could accomplish alone.

No matter what the horse's past has been, God gives us a brand-new day and a brand-new start. The same is true for people.

— John Lyons

Lyons's Christian faith is openly and publicly woven into his work — an aspect that distinguishes him from virtually every other natural horsemanship clinician. He conducts church services at large expositions. He speaks about his faith in his clinics, his books, and his teaching. He sees no separation between his spiritual life and his work with horses and people. "I don't know how to separate Him from my everyday life," he has said, "so it's part of my clinics." This integration — unusual in the horse world — has drawn enormous loyalty from riders who share his faith and find in his teaching not just horsemanship instruction but a broader framework for living.

Publishing & Media

Perfect Horse Magazine and More Than 20 Books

John Lyons founded John Lyons' Perfect Horse magazine — a publication that at its peak reached 110,000 subscribers and became one of the most widely read horse training magazines in North America. The magazine was a natural extension of his clinic philosophy: practical, accessible, grounded in clear methodology, and addressed to the ordinary horse owner rather than the professional competitor.

He has written or co-authored more than twenty books on horses and horsemanship — a body of work that covers the full spectrum of his method from first saddling through advanced training, from foal handling to problem-horse rehabilitation. His landmark book Lyons on Horses remains one of the most complete treatments of his conditioned-response training philosophy, covering every critical aspect of training with an emphasis on communication, understanding, and gentle persuasion over force. He has also produced extensive instructional video libraries.

He has been a keynote speaker at major horse events nationwide and abroad — including the 2010 Alltech FEI World Equestrian Games at the Kentucky Horse Park in Lexington, Kentucky — where he was recognized as one of the figures most responsible for changes in the horse industry over the previous thirty years. He has been honored by breed associations, universities, horse clubs, and magazines for his contributions to horses, riders, and trainers.

Family & Legacy

Our Dream Ranch — and a Family of Horsemen

John Lyons lives with his wife Jody — whom he married in 2003 — at Our Dream Ranch, a 70-acre property in Parachute, Colorado, on the Western Slope between Grand Junction and Glenwood Springs. The property has been his home and base of operations for more than forty years. Together he and Jody have seven children between them.

His children have carried the tradition forward. Son Josh Lyons became a prominent clinician in his own right, working out of Cross Plains, Tennessee, near Nashville, and co-developing the John & Josh Lyons certification program. Son Michael Lyons and daughter Brandi Lyons are also active in the horsemanship world. It is rare to attend a major horse expo today without finding a Lyons — John, Josh, Michael, Brandi, or one of their certified trainers — represented on the program. The family has made horsemanship teaching a multi-generational enterprise.

Lyons has worked with more than 100,000 horses over his career and trained more than 500 professional clinicians. His influence has crossed every discipline and every breed — from the trail rider on a backyard horse to the competitive performer at the highest levels. His definition of success was always the same: a horse that is safe, consistent, and genuinely enjoyable to be around, and a human who understands why — and can reproduce it themselves, without John Lyons in the arena.
