Born to the Rice Dynasty — Spearman Roots, Weatherford Success

Tatum Rice was born in 1985 into a family where horses were not just a profession but an identity. His father Boyd Rice was already building the reputation that would eventually make him a Hall of Famer in both NCHA cutting and NRCHA reined cow horse. His grandfather Sonny Rice was an NCHA World Champion. His great-uncles Ronnie and Raymond Rice were NCHA Hall of Famers. "I've been in cutting forever," Tatum says simply. "My entire life my dad trained cutting horses, and his dad did also."

He began showing cutting horses at age ten — approximately 1995, when the family was still in Spearman, Texas. He rode with his father every day until age seventeen, then moved to work with cousin Tag Rice and great-uncle Ronnie Rice. The education never stopped; it just changed instructors. When he gave up his non-pro card in 2009, he started his professional career at Carl and Shawnea Smith's facility. By 2011, he and his wife Kylie had moved to Weatherford, Texas — the cutting horse capital of the world — and opened T/K Cutting Horses.

We were there every day at the barn. It became our path from early on — we were going to train horses just like him. To us, it's a lifestyle. A hard lifestyle. Long hours, hot days, cold days, sick horses in the middle of the night. It's a lot of work. With all that, it's something that we love.

— Tatum Rice

There is one more family layer worth noting. Tatum's wife Kylie was born Kylie Knight — daughter of Kevin and Sydney Knight. These are the same Kevin and Sydney Knight who gave Tatum's father Boyd the horse Oh Cay N Short ("Coyote") as a Christmas present in 2012, the horse that went on to win the 2014 NRCHA World's Greatest Horseman. The Knights also bred and owned Crey Zee — the mare Tatum rode to the 2018 NCHA Futurity Open Championship. It is that kind of family where the connections run in every direction at once.

"I hope he's not a once-in-a-lifetime horse, but I'll be hard-pressed to have very many more that are better. I hope I have some that are like this in the future, but it's gonna be hard to have that happen."

Hashtags was a blue roan stallion by Metallic Cat out of Dual Rey Tag — a horse described by Tatum as "the whole package: the strength, the feel and the desire." Bred by Jim Haworth and originally owned by Jose Raul Garcia before passing to Hashtag Ventures LP, the stallion announced himself in 2017 with the NCHA Super Stakes Open Championship (score 220) — the same title Tatum's father Boyd had won in 2009 on Third Cutting. Father and son, same event, a decade apart.

In 2018 he took on the NCHA Open World Championship, accumulating so much money across the year's Mercuria/NCHA World Series of Cutting events — Calgary Stampede (228), Idaho, El Rancho, West Texas — that the World Championship was secured before the World Finals even started. At the 2018 World Finals in Fort Worth, they placed in every go-round, and in the fourth and final round, Hashtags closed with a 231 to win the go-round and cap the stallion's career. Total earnings: $476,725.

Rice described his 2018 Idaho Futurity performance as one of the horse's best ever — the stallion had come off a grueling week at the West Texas Futurity, freshened up during a layover in Wyoming, and then delivered a dominant showing in Idaho. That durability and mental toughness, combined with raw athletic talent, is what made Hashtags exceptional. "I hope he's not a once-in-a-lifetime horse," Rice said. "But I'll be hard-pressed to have very many more that are better."

2018 — World + Futurity

The Double — World Champion and Futurity Champion in the Same Year

In 2018, Tatum Rice accomplished something that had been done only once before in recent memory — winning the NCHA Open World Championship and the NCHA Futurity Open Championship in the same calendar year. Austin Shepard had done it in 2017 (World on Deluxe Checks, Futurity on Dual Reyish). Now Tatum matched it: World Champion on Hashtags in early December 2018 at the Fort Worth World Finals, then Futurity Open Champion on Crey Zee just two nights later.

Crey Zee was a mare bred and owned by Kylie's parents, Kevin and Sydney Knight — a personal connection that made the win particularly meaningful. In the Futurity Open finals of 21 entries, Tatum and Crey Zee drew up first and posted a 222 that held all night. Every entry after them came and went without topping it. The score stood as the championship. "I thought I had a shot at it, but I also thought there were four or five other really good horses that had a shot at it, too," Tatum said afterward. Kevin Knight, watching his bred-and-owned mare win, said: "I just felt really good about this horse and Tatum and Kylie, felt like we were going to have a good run. And we had a good run, and I'll be danged if it didn't hold up."

Crey Zee — a Dual Rey mare out of Eazee E (by High Brow Cat) — carried the 2019 NCHA Open Horse of the Year award and $388,194 in career earnings. She was also shown by Kylie Rice in non-pro competition, extending the family connection through both husband and wife competing on the same horse at the highest levels of their respective divisions.
