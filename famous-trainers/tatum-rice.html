Background

Tatum Rice

Tatum Rice was born in 1985 in Spearman, Texas. His father is Boyd Rice, the NCHA Hall of Fame cutting horse trainer. His mother is Halee Rice. His brothers are Tarin Rice (born 1989, 2012 NCHA Open Futurity Co-Champion and NCHA Hall of Fame inductee) and sister Trea Rice (born 1997). Tatum became an NCHA member in 1997. He began showing cutting horses at age 10. He spent six years working for his cousin, Tag Rice. He turned in his non-pro card just before the 2009 NCHA Futurity. He and his wife Kylie Knight Rice — who won the AQHYA Working Cow Horse World Championship in 2005 and 2007 and was the 2006 NRCHA Junior Stock Horse Champion — moved to their own training facility in Weatherford, Texas in 2011. T/K Cutting Horses is their training operation. He later moved to Ardmore, Oklahoma. He has served as an NCHA Director and joined the NCHA Executive Committee in 2017, also serving on the Horse and Cattle Welfare Committee, Amateur Committee, and Limited Age Event Committee.

He has said of his career: "I've been in cutting forever. My entire life my dad [Boyd Rice] trained cutting horses and his dad [Sonny Rice] did."

Career

Competitive Record

At the 2009 NCHA Futurity, Tatum won the Limited Open Reserve Championship on Shes Twice As Smooth. In 2017 he and Hashtags (Metallic Cat × Shining Chex × Shining Spark, owned by Hashtag Ventures LP) scored a 220 to win the NCHA Super Stakes Open finals. Crey Zee was the 2019 NCHA Open Horse of the Year and Tatum won the NCHA Open Futurity Championship on him. In 2025 Tatum was inducted into the NCHA Open Rider Hall of Fame and won the NCHA Open World Championship on Look My Rey (2018 gelding by Dual Rey out of SVR Look Cat Me by Metallic Cat). His NCHA lifetime earnings are approximately $3 million.
