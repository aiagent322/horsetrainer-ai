Background

D. Wayne Lukas

Darrell Wayne Lukas was born on September 2, 1935, in Antigo, Wisconsin, the second of three children. His parents had a farm near Antigo and he grew up with an interest in horses. He earned a master's degree in education from the University of Wisconsin-Madison and then taught at La Crosse Logan High School, where he was head basketball coach. He also worked as an assistant basketball coach at the University of Wisconsin for two years. He switched from Quarter Horses to Thoroughbreds full-time in 1978. His wife was Laurie Lukas; he had a late son, Jeff, and two grandchildren. He was known throughout his career as "The Coach." He died on June 28, 2025, in Louisville, Kentucky, at age 89, after contracting a MRSA blood infection that damaged his heart and digestive system. His family announced his retirement on June 22, 2025.

He said before the 2025 Preakness: "The whole secret of this game, I think, is being able to read the horse: Read what he needs, what he doesn't need, what he can't do, what he can do. That's the whole key."

Career

Racing Record

Lukas retired with 4,953 Thoroughbred wins (9th all-time) and $300,534,643 in career purse earnings (6th all-time). He led North American trainers in earnings 14 of the 15 years beginning in 1983. He was the first trainer to surpass $100 million and $200 million in career purse earnings. He won 15 Triple Crown races: 4 Kentucky Derbies, 7 Preakness Stakes, and 4 Belmont Stakes. He won 20 Breeders' Cup races — a record. He won the Eclipse Award for Outstanding Trainer in 1985, 1986, 1987, and 1994, and the Eclipse Award of Merit in 2013. He was inducted into the National Museum of Racing and Hall of Fame in 1999 and the American Quarter Horse Hall of Fame in 2007 — the first person to be a member of both institutions.

He trained 25 champions: Althea, Azeri, Boston Harbor, Capote, Charismatic, Criminal Type, Family Style, Flanders, Folklore, Golden Attraction, Gulch, Lady's Secret, Landaluce, Life's Magic, North Sider, Open Mind, Orientate, Sacahuista, Serena's Song, Steinlen, Surfside, Take Charge Brandi, Thunder Gulch, Timber Country, and Winning Colors. Three of them were Horse of the Year: Lady's Secret (1986), Criminal Type (1990), and Charismatic (1999). In 1995 he became the first trainer to have multiple horses from his stable win all three Triple Crown races in a single year.

In 2024, at age 88, Lukas became the oldest trainer to win a Triple Crown race when Seize the Grey won the Preakness Stakes — his 7th Preakness win. He mentored trainers including Todd Pletcher, Kiaran McLaughlin, Randy Bradshaw, Mark Hennig, George Weaver, and Dallas Stewart.
